# Evidence Room — Customer & Problem

**Document ID:** `09_RESEARCH/03_CUSTOMER_AND_PROBLEM`  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Define who buys, why they buy, what job they hire Evidence Room to do, and what “success” means in language a Finance Director would recognise.

---

## 1. Primary buyers

| Role | Why they buy | What they fear |
|---|---|---|
| **Head of AP / AP Manager** | Exceptions and supplier noise dominate the day; AI experiments are unmanaged | Shadow agents, broken controls, team distrust |
| **Controller** | Needs audit-defensible design before any autonomy expands | Material weakness narrative; payment risk |
| **CFO / Finance Director** | Wants productivity and control without another multi-year ERP programme | Hype projects that burn trust with the Board |
| **Head of Shared Services / GBS** | Multi-entity AP at scale; needs standard operating model for agents | Fragmented local “AI hacks” |

**Primary economic buyer (Professional / Team):** Finance Director, Controller, or Head of Shared Services who can expense US$199–US$499 without an RFP.

**Primary user:** Head of AP / AP process owner who will run the methodology week one.

Secondary buyers rarely purchase Starter alone; they champion Team or Blueprint.

## 3. Organisation profile (fit)

**Strong fit**
- 200–10,000+ employees  
- Hundreds to tens of thousands of invoices per month  
- ERP-backed AP (Dynamics, SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Workday, or peer)  
- Existing or planned AP automation / RPA / GenAI experiments  
- Control- and audit-sensitive culture (public, PE-backed, regulated, or multi-entity)  
- Leadership mandate to “use AI in finance” without a clear operating model  

**Weak fit**
- Sole-trader / micro-AP with no exception complexity  
- Buyers seeking autonomous payment software  
- Teams wanting ERP replacement or vendor selection RFP services only  
- Prompt tourists seeking novelty content  

*Invoice-volume ranges above are persona design bands — see Research Ledger RL-018 (NON-EVIDENCE). Do not present as surveyed averages.*

## 4. Jobs-to-be-done

### Functional jobs
1. Design which AP work agents may touch — and which they must never touch.  
2. Install a responsibility ladder (L0→L4) with payment authorisation remaining human.  
3. Stand up an exception taxonomy and triage model that reduces firefighting.  
4. Define KPIs, baselines, and evidence packs before claiming “AI success.”  
5. Run a controlled pilot with shadowing, exit criteria, and rollback.  
6. Brief executives and auditors in coherent language.  

### Emotional jobs
- Feel in control of AI adoption rather than chased by it.  
- Avoid being the leader who “let a bot near the payment run.”  
- Show the CFO a Monday-ready plan, not a slide of possibilities.  

### Social jobs
- Be the AP/Finance leader who professionalised agents before peers.  
- Align IT, Audit, and AP on one operating vocabulary.  

---

## 5. Problem statement (as the buyer experiences it)

> We are under pressure to deploy AI in Accounts Payable. Tools and pilots are appearing. What we lack is an **operating system**: who owns each agent, what it is allowed to do, how exceptions are classified, how responsibility is earned, and what evidence proves the design is safe. Software vendors sell features. Prompt packs sell novelty. Consultancies sell projects. We need something we can buy on Friday and use on Monday — without replacing the ERP.

| Trigger | Signal |

| Control / SOX refresh | Need documented AI-related controls before year-end. |

## 7. Objections and answers

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| “We already have AP software.” | Keep it. This is the agent operating layer across your stack. |
| “Is this just prompts?” | No. Charters, controls, KPIs, exception OS, methodology. Prompts are secondary. |
| “Will this pay invoices automatically?” | No. Payment authorisation stays human. Non-negotiable. |
| “We need custom for our ERP.” | Professional/Team are ERP-agnostic by design. Blueprint customises. |
| “Audit will never allow AI.” | Evidence Room exists to make AI *auditable* — ownership, exclusions, evidence packs. |
| “US$199 won’t cover transformation.” | Correct. It covers the operating design. Software and SI remain separate. |
| “We should wait for IT.” | IT builds platforms. You own the AP operating model. Start in parallel. |
| “Show me guaranteed ROI.” | We don’t. We provide measurement design and market context from named sources. |

## 8. Success criteria (buyer-defined)

A purchase of **Professional** is successful when, within one to two working weeks, the buyer can:

1. Name the first 3–5 agents in scope with human owners.  
2. Publish a written responsibility level per agent (starting ≤ L2 unless already evidenced).  
3. Adopt a shared exception taxonomy for the pilot queue.  
4. Baseline 3–5 KPIs (e.g. cycle time, exception rate, STP, cost-to-process if available, inquiry burden).  
5. Complete a shadow or controlled pilot plan with exit criteria.  
6. Brief Controller / Audit on what agents will *not* do.  

A purchase of **Team** is successful when a transformation lead can run a credible internal workshop and leave with assigned actions — not another parking lot.
