# Evidence Room — Competitor Landscape

**Document ID:** `09_RESEARCH/02_COMPETITOR_LANDSCAPE`  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Map adjacent categories so Evidence Room positions as the **agent operating layer**, not as another AP stack, RPA suite, prompt pack, or consulting engagement.  
**Discipline:** Descriptive positioning only. No cloned proprietary screenshots, playbooks, or vendor collateral. Price signals are public-range observations; treat as directional, not quotes.

---

## 1. Positioning thesis (read first)

| Who | What they sell | What buyers still lack |
|---|---|---|
| AP automation platforms | Invoice-to-pay software features | Agent charters, earned autonomy, exception OS, KPI governance across tools |
| ERP finance suites | System of record + native workflows | Lightweight, ERP-agnostic design layer for AI agents |
| RPA vendors | Bots and orchestration | Finance-grade responsibility model and AP domain taxonomy |
| Prompt marketplaces | Disposable prompts | Controls, audit evidence, human ownership, measurement |
| Consulting toolkits / Big 4 methods | Projects and slides | Productised, buyer-owned operating system usable Monday morning |

**Evidence Room white space (defend this relentlessly):**

1. **Agent operating model** — 16 AP agents with jobs, exclusions, owners, escalations.  
2. **Governance** — charters, controls, audit evidence standards.  
3. **Responsibility earning** — L0→L4; full autonomy never default; payment authorisation stays human.  
4. **Exception taxonomy as OS** — structured exception classes, triage, root cause — not a ticket queue bolt-on.  
5. **ERP-agnostic design layer** — works across Dynamics, SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Workday and peers.  

**Not** stack replacement. **Not** autonomous payment software. **Not** a ChatGPT ebook.

## 2. Segment A — AP automation platforms

*Invoice capture, matching, approvals, payments orchestration, supplier portals, analytics. Enterprise and mid-market SaaS.*

### What they sell
Workflow automation for invoice-to-pay: OCR/IDR, PO matching, exception queues, approval routing, supplier networks, sometimes payments and cards.

### Named competitors (illustrative set)
Tipalti · Coupa · Basware · Esker · Yooz · Medius · Stampli · AvidXchange · HighRadius · Ramp · Bill.com · SAP (Invoice Management / Concur-adjacent finance flows) · Oracle (Cloud Payables / Fusion AP) · Microsoft (Dynamics 365 Finance AP)

### Price signals (directional — verify at sale)

| Cluster | Typical commercial pattern |
|---|---|
| Mid-market SaaS (e.g. Bill.com, Stampli, Yooz, Ramp card+bill pay) | Per-user, per-entity, or usage-linked subscriptions; often publicly marketed tiers or freemium card float |
| Specialist AP automation (Tipalti, Medius, Esker, Basware, AvidXchange, HighRadius) | Quote-led; implementation + subscription; multi-year enterprise deals common |
| Suite / ERP modules (Coupa, SAP, Oracle, Microsoft) | Bundle within broader procurement/ERP licence; high switching cost |

Exact list prices change frequently and are often unpublished for enterprise SKUs — **do not invent numbers**.

### Common claims (category pattern)
- Lower cost-per-invoice and faster cycle times via automation  
- Higher straight-through processing / touchless rates  
- AI / ML for coding, matching, anomaly detection  
- Supplier network effects and eInvoicing  
- Audit trails and control features inside the product  

*(Market proof points for cost/time/exception advantages should cite Research Ledger Ardent entries RL-001–RL-008 — not vendor marketing copy.)*

### Gaps relative to Evidence Room
- Softwares optimise **transactions inside one stack**; they rarely sell a portable **agent operating system**.  
- “AI” features are product capabilities — not a **responsibility-earning model** a Controller can defend to audit.  
- Exception handling is usually a **queue**, not a governed **taxonomy + root-cause OS**.  
- Cross-ERP design artefacts (charters, RACI, KPI packs, pilot protocols) are left to the customer or SI.  
- No productised Friday→Monday path for redesigning the *agent layer* without a software RFP.

### Evidence Room stance
Complement, do not compete head-on. Buyer keeps Tipalti/Coupa/SAP/etc.; Evidence Room designs how agents behave **across** that estate.

## 3. Segment B — RPA and hyperautomation platforms

### Named competitors
UiPath · Automation Anywhere (AA) · (adjacent: Microsoft Power Automate, Blue Prism/SS&C where still present)

### What they sell
Bot builders, orchestrators, document understanding, process mining connectors, AI centre modules, attended/unattended automation.

### Price signals
Enterprise licence + bot runners + orchestration capacity; quote-led. Citizen-developer SKUs exist but serious AP automation is still a programme cost.

### Common claims
- Automate repetitive AP keystrokes and email handling  
- Scale digital workers; integrate with ERP UIs and APIs  
- Accelerate ROI vs. headcount  

### Gaps relative to Evidence Room
- RPA is a **delivery substrate**, not an AP **operating model**.  
- Bots without charters create shadow process and control risk.  
- No native L0→L4 responsibility ladder tied to payment-sensitive work.  
- Exception taxonomy and KPI evidence packs are customer-built.

### Evidence Room stance
Where RPA exists, Evidence Room specifies **which agent responsibilities may be bot-backed**, under what controls, and how autonomy is earned. Evidence Room is not a UiPath competitor.

## 4. Segment C — Prompt marketplaces and GenAI wrappers

### What they sell
Prompt packs, GPT wrappers, “AI for finance” Notion templates, Discord/Gumroad bundles, generic agent builders with no AP control design.

### Price signals
Typically US$9–US$199 digital downloads; subscription wrappers vary. Low trust barrier; low audit defensibility.

### Common claims
- “100 prompts for AP”  
- Instant productivity; replace analysts  
- Autonomous agents / “set and forget”

### Gaps relative to Evidence Room
- No human accountability design; payment risk casually ignored.  
- No audit evidence standards, escalation paths, or KPI baselines.  
- No methodology (Observe → Expand responsibility).  
- Brand aesthetic usually hype-coded — opposite of CFO trust.

### Evidence Room stance
Category foil. Explicit anti-positioning: Evidence Room is **not** a prompt pack. Starter Kit may include prompt *patterns*, always nested under charters and controls.

## 5. Segment D — Consulting toolkits and transformation methods

### What they sell
AP diagnostic workshops, target operating models, SI implementations, Big 4 / mid-tier transformation programmes, vendor-funded “value engineering.”

### Price signals
Day rates and SOWs; AP automation programmes commonly five to seven figures all-in when software + SI are combined. Productised digital kits at Evidence Room’s price points (US$79–US$499) are rare at comparable depth.

### Common claims
- End-to-end transformation  
- Best-practice process redesign  
- Change management and benefits realisation  

### Gaps relative to Evidence Room
- Knowledge leaves with the engagement.  
- Artefacts often slideware, not buyer-owned operating OS.  
- Slow to mobilise; procurement friction for a Controller who needs Monday traction.  
- Incentive to expand scope into software selection / build.

### Evidence Room stance
Team + Custom Blueprint **productise** the front of the funnel. Consultancies remain partners for large ERP programmes; Evidence Room is the reusable method layer the buyer keeps.

## 7. Objection handling (competitive)

| Buyer says | Evidence Room response |
|---|---|
| “We already have Coupa / Tipalti / SAP.” | Good — keep it. Evidence Room designs the agent operating layer *on top of* that stack. |
| “IT is building agents in UiPath / Copilot.” | Then they need charters, exclusions, L0→L4 gates, and exception taxonomy — before scale. |
| “We bought a prompt pack.” | Prompts without ownership and controls are a control finding waiting to happen. |
| “We’ll hire consultants.” | Do. Use Team / Blueprint so the method stays when the SOW ends. |
| “Is this software?” | No. Implementation-ready operating system toolkit. ERP-agnostic. No autonomous payments. |
