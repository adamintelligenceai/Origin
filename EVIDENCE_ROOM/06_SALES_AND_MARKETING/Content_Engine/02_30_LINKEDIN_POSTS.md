## Post 1 — Category
Most “AI in AP” programmes fail the Control conversation before they fail the model.

Not because the OCR is weak.  
Because nobody wrote down what the agent may do, what it must never do, who owns it, and how responsibility is earned.

Software vendors sell features.  
Prompt packs sell novelty.  
What Finance still lacks is an **operating system for agents**.

That’s the gap Evidence Room exists to fill: operating evidence before autonomy expands.

---

## Post 2 — Misconception
The biggest misconception in Accounts Payable AI:

“We’ll know it’s working when the bot pays faster.”

No.

You’ll know the design is serious when:
- every agent has a named human owner  
- exclusions are written  
- exception classes are shared  
- responsibility is capped (L0–L4)  
- payment authorisation remains human  

Speed without evidence is just a future audit finding.

## Post 3 — First agents
If you charter only four AP agents this quarter, charter these:

1. Invoice Intake  
2. Invoice Validation  
3. Matching  
4. Exception Triage  

Leave Payment Proposal Review human-gated.  
Leave “full autonomy” off the slide.

Earn the right to expand. Don’t announce it.

---

## Post 4 — Responsibility ladder
Responsibility model worth putting on the wall:

L0 Observe  
L1 Recommend  
L2 Prepare  
L3 Execute within guardrails  
L4 Managed autonomy (earned only)

Full autonomy is never the default.  
Payments stay human.

If your pilot starts at L3 because a vendor demo looked smooth, you don’t have a pilot — you have a hope.

---

## Post 5 — Exceptions
Your exception queue is not a strategy.

An **exception taxonomy** is.

When every mismatch is “Other,” you cannot triage, you cannot root-cause, and you cannot teach an agent what “good” means.

Classify first. Automate second.

---

## Post 6 — Vs prompts
A prompt is not a job description.  
A job description is not a control.  
A control is not evidence.  
Evidence is not permission to expand autonomy.

Most marketplace “AP prompt packs” stop at step one and market it as transformation.

Controllers can tell the difference.

## Post 7 — ERP agnostic
You do not need a new ERP to professionalise AP agents.

You need:
- charters  
- owners  
- escalation paths  
- KPI baselines  
- shadow → pilot gates  

Dynamics, SAP, Oracle, NetSuite, Workday — the operating layer should travel.

Rip-and-replace is rarely the first honest move.

## Post 8 — Audit
Internal Audit will not ask whether your demo was impressive.

They will ask:
- Who can change the agent’s scope?  
- What evidence shows segregation of duties still holds?  
- What happens when the agent fails closed vs fails open?  
- Who authorised the responsibility level?

Design for those questions on day one.

A useful test for any AP AI toolkit:

If the answer is “we need a 12-week discovery,” you bought a project, not an operating system.

## Post 11 — Intake
Invoice Intake agents should classify and route.

They should not quietly become posting engines.

The charter’s exclusions section matters more than the clever prompt.

Write the exclusions first. You’ll sleep better.

---

## Post 12 — Matching
Matching agents are only as trustworthy as the evidence they surface.

“Match failed” is not an output standard.  
“Match failed — PO line 40 qty 10 vs GR qty 8 — evidence pack attached” is.

Force the agent to show its working. That’s operating evidence.

---

## Post 13 — Supplier resolution
Supplier resolution without ownership becomes inbox theatre.

Charter the agent to prepare the chase, draft the ask, log the timeline — and keep commercial commitment human.

Tone can be automated. Authority cannot.

---

## Post 14 — Duplicate anomaly
Duplicate & anomaly detection is not the same as payment blocking.

Detection recommends.  
Policy decides.  
Payment authorisation stays with people.

Conflating those three is how “AI fraud prevention” claims get oversold.

---

## Post 15 — KPIs
If you cannot baseline it, you cannot claim the agent improved it.

Minimum KPI set before any “AI success” narrative:
- cycle time (defined)  
- exception rate (defined)  
- STP / touchless (defined)  
- inquiry volume  
- rework rate  

Blank cells are allowed. Fiction is not.

---

## Post 16 — Shadow mode
Shadow mode is not delay. It is respect for the payment run.

Observe → recommend → compare to human decisions → measure disagreement → only then consider raising responsibility.

Skipping shadow to “move fast” is how you move backwards with Audit.

---

## Post 17 — Orchestrator
An AP Manager / Orchestrator agent is not a boss. It is a traffic system.

It sequences work, surfaces conflicts, and escalates.  
It does not replace the Head of AP’s accountability.

If your architecture chart shows the orchestrator above the human owner, redraw it.

## Post 21 — PO quality
PO Quality agents prevent AP pain upstream.

If your invoice exceptions are mostly “should have been caught at PO,” stop asking AP to heroically clean finance’s mess with a chatbot.

Charter the upstream agent. Measure preventable exceptions.

---

## Post 22 — Approval
Approval agents should accelerate complete packets — not rubber-stamp incomplete ones.

Output standard:  
packet completeness score + missing artefacts list + policy references.

Anything less trains the organisation to approve theatre.

---

## Post 23 — Close & reporting
AP Close and AP Reporting agents are where “productivity AI” becomes “control AI.”

If close checklists and reconciliations get agent support, document the evidence standard like you would for a human preparer — because Audit will.

---

## Post 24 — Root cause
Triage without root cause is forever firefighting.

A Root Cause agent that only writes witty summaries is entertainment.  
A Root Cause agent that tags systemic drivers (PO quality, GR lag, supplier master, policy ambiguity) is an operating system component.

Demand tags. Demand trends. Demand owners.

---

## Post 25 — Cost monitoring
Every agent has a cost: model calls, review time, false positive load, exception spillover.

If nobody monitors cost-to-serve for the agent, you may “save” FTE minutes and burn them in silent review tax.

Put cost monitoring in the charter. It’s not optional finance hygiene.
