## Cover and positioning

Most finance teams have already bought something that extracts invoices, routes approvals, or “uses AI.” That is not the question this diagnostic answers.

The question is: **can this AP operation produce evidence that would justify giving an agent any responsibility at all?**

Evidence Room treats agents as tools with job descriptions. They start at observe. They move only when a named human can show precision, miss rate, exception cost, and a kill-switch. This file is the first measurement of whether that discipline is even possible in your shop.

**What this diagnostic is for**

- A CFO who will trade an email address for a clear picture of AP readiness — not a vendor demo.
- A Controller who needs to know whether the control environment would survive an agent touching invoices.
- An AP Manager who must pick one bounded first agent, not sixteen.

**What you will walk away with**

1. A transparent score out of 144, mapped to a five-level maturity model.
2. Six domain scores so a high “AI enthusiasm” score cannot hide a weak control or data score.
3. An opportunity heatmap you plot from *your* volume, pain, control risk, and data readiness — not from a vendor ranking.
4. A first-wave view of ten agents (the subset of the sixteen-agent stack you would consider first).
5. A baseline KPI worksheet with blanks. You fill them. We do not invent industry targets.
6. A business-case starter that structures conservative / base / upside *inputs*. It does not compute or promise savings.

**Voice of this product.** Intelligent, concise, executive, specific. No hype. No guaranteed savings, fraud detection, regulatory compliance, accounting accuracy, autonomous payment safety, or ROI. ## How to complete (30–40 minutes)

**What to do.** Score 36 questions across six domains. Enter your own baseline KPIs. Plot three opportunities on the heatmap. Stop. Do not design an agent in this sitting.

**How.**

1. Appoint a **scorer** (Head of AP or AP Manager) and a **challenge** (Controller or Control owner). One person scoring alone produces optimism.
2. Open this file and, if you have it, the companion workbook described in `SCORECARD_INSTRUCTIONS.md`.
3. Work domain by domain. For each question pick **one** integer 0–4 using the rubric. If you are between two scores, take the lower one.
4. Write a one-line evidence note: the report, queue, SOP version, or “we do not have this.” A score without a note is a guess. Guessing is allowed — mark it `G`.
5. Do not workshop the answers. If two people disagree by two or more points, record both and use the lower score. The disagreement *is* the finding.
6. Complete the baseline KPI worksheet with blanks or “unknown.” Unknown is a valid answer and lowers Data & systems and Agent readiness when those questions ask about measurement.
7. Plot the heatmap after scoring, not before. The score tells you whether you are allowed to get excited about an opportunity.
8. Timebox: 25 minutes on questions, 8 minutes on KPIs and heatmap, 5 minutes on interpretation. If you overrun, you are designing, not diagnosing.

**Who.**

| Role | Duty |
|---|---|
| Head of AP (scorer) | Answers process, exception, talent, and agent questions |
| Controller or Control owner (challenge) | Answers control and audit questions; vetoes inflated scores |
| Finance Systems (optional, 10 minutes) | Confirms data, interface, and identity facts |
| CFO (recipient) | Reads the interpretation page, not the 36 rubrics |

**What can go wrong.**

- Scoring the SOP instead of the floor. The rubric is about what operators *do*.
- Letting a capture-vendor dashboard substitute for AP’s own evidence.
- Scoring “we bought AI” as Agent readiness. A licence is not a charter.
- Completing it after a vendor demo. Recency bias will push every score up one point.

**Control.** Challenge initials the cover. Any domain averaging ≥ 3.0 must cite artefacts, not memory. Guess-marked items cannot be the sole support for a “ready for agents” conclusion.

**Measure.** Minutes to complete; count of `G` (guess) notes; number of two-point disagreements.

**Evidence.** Dated score sheet (this file or the workbook), initials, and the baseline KPI page. Store under `[BUYER]/Evidence/Diagnostic/`.

**ACME — ILLUSTRATIVE.** Head of AP and Controller sit for 38 minutes. They disagree on CA-03 (duplicate control) by two points; they take 1. Twelve answers are marked `G`. The CFO receives a one-page interpretation, not a 16-agent roadmap.

## Scoring method (transparent, not a black box)

**What to do.** Convert 36 integer answers into six domain scores and one overall score. Map the overall average to a maturity level. Do not invent weights.

**How.**

| Object | Formula |
|---|---|
| Question score | Integer 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4. No halves. |
| Domain score | Sum of its 6 questions ÷ 6. Report to one decimal. |
| Overall score | Sum of all 36 questions ÷ 36. Report to one decimal. |
| Overall points | Sum of all 36 questions. Maximum 144. |
| Maturity level | Look up overall score in the maturity table below. |

There are **no hidden weights**. Process integrity does not outrank Controls. A shop that scores 3.8 on Agent readiness and 1.1 on Controls is not “almost ready.” It is Experimental, because the overall average will say so — and because the interpretation rules treat a Controls domain below 2.0 as a **hard brake** on any execute-level ambition.

**Confidence tag (optional, does not change the score).**

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `E` | Evidence on file (report, extract, SOP version, access list) |
| `O` | Observed on the floor in the last 90 days |
| `G` | Guess / tribal knowledge |

If more than 12 answers are `G`, the diagnostic is **indicative only**. Re-run after a two-hour evidence pull. Do not take an indicative result to a steering committee as a decision.

**Who.** Scorer computes. Challenge checks arithmetic and the hard-brake rule.

**What can go wrong.** Averaging only the high domains. Re-weighting “because AI is strategic.” Converting 4s into a percentage that looks like an exam grade.

**Control.** Publish all six domain scores next to the overall score. Never publish overall alone.

**Measure.** Domain range (max − min). A range ≥ 2.0 is a finding: the operation is uneven, not “average 2.4.”

**Evidence.** The arithmetic is this section. A workbook that changes weights is not this diagnostic.

## The 36 questions

Score each item 0–4 using the AP-specific rubric. “Why it matters” is the reason a CFO should care — not a prompt to buy software.

### Domain 1 — Process integrity

*Does a second person know how an invoice actually moves — and would an agent be automating the real path or the poster?*

#### PI-01 — Documented as-done path

**Question.** Is there a current, signed description of how invoices move from receipt to ready-for-payment *as operators do it*, including informal queues?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | No SOP, or the SOP is a vendor implementation leftover. Operators cannot point to it. |
| **1** | SOP exists; last review > 18 months; floor uses email folders and side spreadsheets the SOP omits. |
| **2** | SOP current for the happy path. Exception and payment-prep paths are tribal. |
| **3** | As-done map covers every live channel and the top exception types. Informal workarounds are listed, not hidden. |
| **4** | As-done map versioned, signed by Head of AP, sampled by Controls in the last 12 months. Floor and document match on a live walkthrough. |

**Why it matters.** An agent that follows the SOP while clerks follow Jane’s spreadsheet will create a second, uncontrolled path.

#### PI-02 — Channel inventory

**Question.** Can you name every inbound channel (mailbox, portal, EDI/cXML, scan, punch-out, shared inbox) and the volume on each for the last complete quarter?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | “Invoices come in by email.” No mailbox list. No volume split. |
| **1** | Main mailbox known. Portal and EDI exist but are not counted. Plant inboxes are unofficial. |
| **2** | Channels listed. Volume is a guess or a vendor dashboard that does not reconcile to ERP receipts. |
| **3** | Channel list with quarterly counts that reconcile to invoices received in ERP / workflow (± a documented residual). |
| **4** | Channel list owned, volume reconciled, and each channel has a named intake owner and a fallback if that channel dies. |

**Why it matters.** First-wave agents fail on the channel nobody listed. Volume without a channel split makes a heatmap fiction.

#### PI-03 — PO vs non-PO discipline

**Question.** Is it unambiguous, in policy *and* in the queue, which invoices require a PO, and what happens when they do not have one?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | “Most things should have a PO.” Clerks decide case by case. |
| **1** | Policy exists. After-the-fact POs are routine and unflagged. |
| **2** | PO-required categories written. Non-PO path exists but is used as a bypass. |
| **3** | PO-required flag is system-enforced by category or vendor. Retro-POs are flagged and reported. |
| **4** | Policy, system flag, retro-PO report, and a named Procurement owner for repeat bypass. Non-PO is a designed path, not a leak. |

**Why it matters.** Missing-PO is usually a high-volume exception. If policy is soft, an agent will either invent a PO or rubber-stamp a bypass.

#### PI-04 — Match rule is written

**Question.** Are 2-way / 3-way / tolerance rules written as a table an auditor could test — not “we match when it looks right”?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Matching is judgement. Tolerances live in someone’s head. |
| **1** | ERP has a tolerance. Nobody can produce the current values and who last changed them. |
| **2** | Tolerance table exists. Service POs, blanket POs, and multi-line invoices are handled by exception folklore. |
| **3** | Documented match policy: which invoice types are 2-way vs 3-way, numeric tolerances, who may widen them, and that widening is a change — not a clerk action. |
| **4** | Policy version cited on every exception. Config export matches the document. Changes go through a change record. |

**Why it matters.** A language model must not be the match engine. If the rule is unpublished, you cannot tell whether the agent or the human is guessing.

#### PI-05 — Exception language

**Question.** Do parked invoices use a finite, shared set of reason codes, or a comment field?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Free-text holds. “Waiting,” “check,” “supplier.” |
| **1** | ERP reason codes exist. >30% of volume is Other / Misc / blank. |
| **2** | Codes used on most items. Same break has three names across teams. |
| **3** | Published taxonomy (or a mapped ERP list). Primary code required. Misc has a weekly cap review. |
| **4** | Taxonomy versioned; ERP mapped 1:1; reports run by code, owner, and age — not by “exceptions.” |

**Why it matters.** Agents route by code. A comment field cannot own an SLA.

#### PI-06 — Close and cutoff are designed

**Question.** Is period-end for AP a checklist with owners (accruals, GR/IR, unrecorded spend, held payments), or a heroic weekend?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Close is “get through the pile.” No checklist. |
| **1** | Checklist exists. Half the items are informal. GR/IR is a surprise every month. |
| **2** | Checklist used. Accruals depend on one person. Statement gaps are optional. |
| **3** | Named close owner, checklist, GR/IR ageing reviewed, unrecorded-spend scan defined. |
| **4** | Close pack retained; cutoff rules written; agent-touched items cannot skip the checklist. |

**Why it matters.** An agent that posts faster into a sloppy cutoff moves the error into the next period. Speed is not close quality.

**Domain 1 evidence note.** Artefacts that support a high score: as-done map, channel volume extract, PO-required policy, match-tolerance export, reason-code mix, close checklist.

### Domain 2 — Data & systems

*Can you read a trustworthy invoice, vendor, PO, GR, and payment record — and write evidence — without inventing fields?*

#### DS-01 — Invoice identity

**Question.** Is there a stable key for “this invoice” across channel, workflow, and ERP (supplier + invoice number + entity, or equivalent), and do you know how duplicates of the *key* are treated?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Invoice number is whatever was typed. Spaces and prefixes create new documents. |
| **1** | ERP has a reference field. Normalisation is tribal (“strip INV-”). |
| **2** | Key rule written. Not applied to portal vs email copies of the same invoice. |
| **3** | Documented key, normalisation, and collision behaviour. Workflow and ERP share the key. |
| **4** | Key rule versioned; tested against known resends; used before payment proposal. |

**Why it matters.** Duplicate control and cycle-time clocks both die without a stable identity.

#### DS-02 — Master-data fitness

**Question.** Can AP trust vendor, entity, tax standing, and payment-term data enough to validate an invoice — and is AP forbidden from editing payment destination from the invoice?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Clerks create or edit vendors to “get it through.” Bank details copied from PDFs. |
| **1** | Steward exists. AP still applies one-off vendor fixes. Bank changes arrive as invoice footnotes. |
| **2** | Create/edit is ticketed. Bank-change path exists but is sometimes skipped under urgency. |
| **3** | Steward-only writes. Invoice-printed IBANs cannot update the master. Dual control on bank changes. |
| **4** | Steward-only writes, dual control evidenced, AP identity cannot write payment destination, and blocked/incomplete vendors fail closed. |

**Why it matters.** The classic loss path is payment-master change, not OCR. Data readiness includes *who may write*, not just completeness percentages.

#### DS-03 — PO and GR readability

**Question.** Can a processor (or an agent at L0) read remaining PO quantity/value, GR quantity, and status *for the invoice’s entity and supplier* without a side spreadsheet?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Match is visual. Remaining value is phoned to the buyer. |
| **1** | ERP shows PO and GR. Interface lag is unknown. Plant receipts live in a warehouse system AP cannot see. |
| **2** | Reads work for stock POs. Service confirmations and blanket POs are opaque. |
| **3** | Documented objects for PO remaining, GR cumulative, status. Known interface lag is written (and treated as a fail-closed if stale). |
| **4** | Reads are API- or report-stable; heartbeat on GR/PO interfaces; stale feed stops match — it does not guess. |

**Why it matters.** Matching on a stale GR extract fabricates “received.” That is not a model problem; it is a data-trust problem.

#### DS-04 — Evidence store

**Question.** When an invoice is parked, matched, or paid, can you retrieve the source file, the extract, the decision, and who overrode what — without asking a vendor to “pull a log”?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | PDF lives in email. ERP has a document number. No join. |
| **1** | Archive exists. Search is by date. Override history is a comment. |
| **2** | Source attached in workflow. Agent or OCR traces live only at the vendor. |
| **3** | Buyer-controlled store: source, hash or equivalent, extract, decision, actor. Retention follows finance policy. |
| **4** | Store is the system of record for evidence; vendor traces are copies; agents cannot delete; export tested. |

**Why it matters.** Permission to act requires proof. If proof evaporates with a vendor contract, you cannot promote an agent and you cannot defend a close.

#### DS-05 — Integration honesty

**Question.** Are capture, workflow, ERP, tax engine, and bank/payment file *distinct* systems with known owners — or a pile of CSVs and a shared inbox that “is the integration”?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | People are the interface. CSV on desktops. |
| **1** | Some APIs or IDocs. Failures discovered when suppliers chase. |
| **2** | Interfaces listed. No heartbeat. Reruns can duplicate. |
| **3** | Interface register: source, target, owner, failure behaviour, at-most-once note for anything that creates a document. |
| **4** | Heartbeats, fail-closed on stale data, duplicate-safe reruns, owner on-call for payment week. |

**Why it matters.** Agents amplify a bad interface. A retry that creates a second ERP invoice is a control incident, not an IT ticket.

#### DS-06 — Measurement extract

**Question.** Can you produce, from systems you control, last-quarter counts for invoices received, posted, exceptions by reason, cycle time, and open aged items — without a project?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Volume is “busy.” No extract. |
| **1** | Vendor dashboard. Does not reconcile to ERP. |
| **2** | ERP counts exist. Exception mix and cycle time need a one-off query. |
| **3** | Repeatable extract: volume, mix, ageing, cycle-time clock defined (receipt → ready-for-payment or equivalent). |
| **4** | Repeatable extract, clock definition versioned, mix (channel × type × entity) available, used monthly. |

**Why it matters.** The baseline KPI worksheet is empty without this. You cannot later claim improvement you cannot measure now.

**Domain 2 evidence note.** Artefacts: invoice-key rule, vendor/bank SoD extract, PO/GR data dictionary, archive pointer, interface register, last-quarter volume extract.

### Domain 3 — Controls & audit

*Would Internal Audit rather sample this operation — or write a finding the week an agent posts its first invoice?*

#### CA-01 — Delegation of authority is a table

**Question.** Is DoA a current table (role, entity, amount, category) that workflow actually uses — not a PDF in a drawer?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Approvals are “whoever is around.” |
| **1** | DoA PDF exists. Workflow limits are stale. Self-approval happens. |
| **2** | Table in workflow. Deputies are informal out-of-office texts. |
| **3** | Versioned DoA; workflow cites it; expired delegates fail; self-approval blocked. |
| **4** | Controller-owned table; change-controlled; sample of approvals vs table in the last 12 months. |

**Why it matters.** An approval agent may route. It must not become the approver. If DoA is mush, routing only accelerates the wrong signature.

#### CA-02 — Segregation of duties on money

**Question.** Are invoice entry, vendor-bank change, and payment release incompatible duties — for people *and* for any service account already in use?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Same person can enter, change bank, and release. Or a shared login does. |
| **1** | Policy says SoD. Shared mailbox or “AP robot” account has combined rights. |
| **2** | Human SoD holds. Project/service accounts have not been reviewed. |
| **3** | Human and system identities reviewed. Incompatible pairs listed. Exceptions have expiry. |
| **4** | Quarterly access cert includes agent/service identities. Payment-release verb cannot sit on a capture identity. |

**Why it matters.** Standing up an agent on a privileged token collapses SoD in an afternoon.

#### CA-03 — Duplicate control before cash

**Question.** Is exact-key duplicate checking performed before post *and* again before the payment proposal — and is a paid collision treated as an incident?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Clerks remember invoices. No systematic check. |
| **1** | ERP blocks exact same reference sometimes. Portal and email copies get through. |
| **2** | Check at entry. Payment run is not re-checked. Near-duplicates are ignored or guessed. |
| **3** | Exact-key rule documented; run at entry and at proposal; paid collision has an incident path. Near-duplicates are a *flag*, not a fraud label. |
| **4** | Both gates evidenced; false-negative sample includes paid population; near-duplicate review is owned. |

**Why it matters.** This is not a fraud-detection claim. It is a payment-hygiene control. Missing it is a hard brake on execute-level agents.

#### CA-04 — Bank-change path

**Question.** Can a new IBAN or account on an invoice, email, or portal message *ever* update the payment destination without an official dual-control path?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Clerks update bank details from the PDF “to help the supplier.” |
| **1** | Policy forbids it. Urgency overrides it. No hold code. |
| **2** | Ticket path exists. Invoice-footer changes still happen. |
| **3** | Document-driven bank write is impossible in the system AP uses. Hold is mandatory. Out-of-band verification per Treasury policy. |
| **4** | Technical impossibility + hold + dual control + incident class if anyone acted on document text. |

**Why it matters.** Payment destination is a human class at every autonomy level in Evidence Room. If the path is porous, do not add an agent that reads invoice text.

#### CA-05 — Override and hold hygiene

**Question.** When someone bypasses a match, a hold, or a workflow step, is it logged with a reason, a person, and a sample review — or is “force through” the unofficial SOP?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Force-post / lift-hold with no log. |
| **1** | Comments. No report. |
| **2** | System logs exist. Nobody reads them. Force-path is how month-end works. |
| **3** | Reason codes; named roles for force-path; weekly override rate visible to AP Manager. |
| **4** | Override sampled; force-path dual-controlled above a cap; rising override rate freezes any automation change. |

**Why it matters.** An agent at L3 with a culture of force-path is an agent whose gates will be walked around.

#### CA-06 — Audit trail usable by a stranger

**Question.** Could Internal Audit, given an invoice ID, reconstruct source → extract → match/exception → approval → payment recommendation → release, with actors and timestamps, in one sitting?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | No. Evidence is email. |
| **1** | ERP document flow only. Capture and approval live elsewhere with different keys. |
| **2** | Most steps findable. Overrides and vendor-portal copies are the gaps. |
| **3** | End-to-end join documented. A stranger can follow a sample of 10. |
| **4** | Join tested in the last audit cycle; gaps logged as findings with owners; agent actions (if any) already in the same trail. |

**Why it matters.** If a human trail is incomplete, an agent trail will not magically appear.

**Domain 3 evidence note.** Artefacts: DoA version, SoD pair list + access extract, duplicate rule, bank-change SOP, override report, audit sample pack.

### Domain 4 — Exception economics

*Do you know what breaks, what it costs in time, who owns it, and whether the same break repeats — or is “exceptions” a single tired pile?*

#### EE-01 — Volume and mix

**Question.** For the last complete quarter, can you state exception volume and the mix of the top codes (or your local reason codes)?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Unknown. The queue is “the queue.” |
| **1** | Headcount feel. “Mostly GR.” No count. |
| **2** | Total exception count. Mix is a pie chart from a vendor with different names. |
| **3** | Count and mix from *your* codes for a quarter, same scope (entity/channel). |
| **4** | Mix by code, owner, channel, entity; trend vs prior quarter; Misc under a cap. |

**Why it matters.** First-wave agents should attack a named code family, not “AP.” Mix is how you pick.

#### EE-02 — Ageing is owned

**Question.** Are open exceptions aged by code and owner, with a named person for items past your own clock?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | No ageing. Old items sit in personal folders. |
| **1** | Ageing report exists. Nobody is assigned. Month-end mass-closes occur. |
| **2** | Age buckets. Ownership is the last person who touched it. |
| **3** | Age × code × owner. Escalation clock written. Mass-close is a control event. |
| **4** | Ageing reviewed weekly; stalled paths get a second code (aged unresolved); write-off/reject above cap is human. |

**Why it matters.** Agents that “clear ageing” by closing as no-issue are a control failure. You need the clock before you automate the chase.

#### EE-03 — Time on task

**Question.** Do you have *any* measured minutes (time study, timestamps, or sampling) for intake, match, and the top two exception types?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | No. Cost is headcount divided by hope. |
| **1** | A vendor slide with “industry minutes.” Not your clocks. |
| **2** | One-off sample > 12 months old, happy path only. |
| **3** | Dated sample or system timestamps for intake + one high-volume exception, method written. |
| **4** | Repeatable timestamps; review/override minutes included; used in a cost model the Controller will look at. |

**Why it matters.** Hours-released arithmetic without a time study is storytelling. This diagnostic will not invent a minute standard.

#### EE-04 — Repeat rate

**Question.** Do you know which supplier × exception-code (or buyer × code) pairs recur, and does anyone own a fix request?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Every invoice is new. |
| **1** | People “know” the bad suppliers. No list. |
| **2** | Informal watchlist. No fix owner. |
| **3** | Repeat report for a look-back you define. Top pairs have a named buyer or process owner. |
| **4** | Repeat report drives a fix request with evidence; recoding to hide repeats is sampled. |

**Why it matters.** Root-cause work is how volume falls. An agent that chases the same missing GR 200 times is a very expensive printer.

#### EE-05 — Owner per break

**Question.** For missing PO, price variance, missing GR, duplicate, tax, and coding, is there a responsible *role* (not “AP will chase”)?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | AP owns everything by default. |
| **1** | Unofficial: warehouse for GR, buyer for price — when they answer. |
| **2** | RACI poster. Live queue still dumps to AP. |
| **3** | Routing table: code → role → next action → clock. AP coordinates; does not absorb. |
| **4** | Table versioned; reject-to-sender when the owner is wrong; AP Manager reviews unowned aged items. |

**Why it matters.** Exception Triage is useless without a routing table. Talent problems often hide here.

#### EE-06 — Unit cost honesty

**Question.** Do you have a Controller-acceptable view of what AP processing costs (people, vendors, allocations) per invoice or per exception — even if rough?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | No cost model. |
| **1** | Benchmark from a conference. Not your ledger. |
| **2** | Headcount ÷ invoices. Ignores vendor, overtime, and plant AP. |
| **3** | Written cost model: components listed, volume milestone defined, Controller has seen it. |
| **4** | Versioned model used quarterly; inference/tool cost has a line if tools exist; no unsigned “savings” on the same page. |

**Why it matters.** APQC’s published spread ($0.38 vs $0.92 per $1,000 revenue to process AP) is *their* research, not your target. Your business case starts from *your* cost model. If you do not have one, the business-case starter stays at inputs-only.

**Domain 4 evidence note.** Artefacts: exception mix extract, ageing snapshot, time-study ID, repeat report, routing table, cost-model version.

### Domain 5 — Talent & operating model

*Is there a human operating system an agent could join — or would the agent join a hero culture?*

#### TO-01 — Named roles, not heroes

**Question.** Are Head of AP, AP Manager, exception owners, payment preparer, payment authoriser, GR owner, buyer, and master-data steward identifiable people (or role pools) with deputies?

**Why it matters.** Deloitte’s 2026 survey found 63% of finance teams have fully deployed and actively use AI, while only 21% report clear, measurable ROI. Supervision skill is part of why those numbers diverge. That is their finding, not a promise that supervision produces ROI.

### Domain 6 — Agent readiness

*Have you earned the right to give a tool a job — or are you scoring a slide?*

#### AR-01 — Charter thinking

**Question.** For any existing bot, OCR, or “AI invoice” tool, is there a written job: in-scope, exclusions, owner, and what it must not do (pay, change bank, approve, invent GR)?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Tools have slogans. No job description. |
| **1** | Vendor scope statement. No exclusions. No human owner. |
| **2** | Internal owner. Exclusions implied. Payment and bank not explicitly forbidden. |
| **3** | One-pager: purpose, exclusions including the four human classes, owner, start-in-observe. |
| **4** | Charter-grade document; tools/entitlements listed; used in access reviews. |

**Why it matters.** If today’s tool has no job description, tomorrow’s agent will inherit the same vagueness.

#### AR-02 — Observe-first discipline

**Question.** Have you ever run a new AP automation in shadow (observe / recommend only) against labelled outcomes before giving it a write?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Go-live is the test. |
| **1** | Vendor UAT with happy-path samples. |
| **2** | Pilot on live invoices with write access “limited.” No labelled miss log. |
| **3** | Shadow or labelled historical pack before write. Precision and misses recorded. |
| **4** | Observe → recommend → prepare is policy. Skipping a level requires a recorded refusal of this diagnostic’s intent — and should not happen. |

**Why it matters.** Evidence Room default is L0 Observe. A culture that cannot shadow cannot promote.

#### AR-03 — Human classes understood

**Question.** Is it explicit, in writing, that payment release, vendor bank-change, policy exceptions, and legal disputes stay human — regardless of model confidence?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Not discussed. “If the AI is sure…” is heard in the corridor. |
| **1** | Payment release is human. The other three are assumed. |
| **2** | All four stated in a slide. Not in a control or SOP. |
| **3** | Written rule. Urgency does not move a case across the line. Confidence is not a second signature. |
| **4** | Written rule plus reason codes that force `human_required`. Sampled. |

**Why it matters.** This is the cheapest control you can install today. It does not require software.

#### AR-04 — Kill-switch and identity

**Question.** Can a named human disable a capture bot, workflow automation, or service account *today* without opening a two-week ticket — and do you know which identity it uses?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Unknown identity. Vendor must be called. |
| **1** | Identity known. Disable path is theoretical. |
| **2** | FinSys can disable. AP Manager cannot trigger it. Untested. |
| **3** | Named kill-switch owners (AP Manager and Controls). Identity inventory exists. |
| **4** | Drill in the last six months; disable minutes recorded; scope can be stack / tool / vendor / entity. |

**Why it matters.** L3 without a kill-switch is not managed autonomy. Even L0 tools should be disable-able.

#### AR-05 — Prompt and document distrust

**Question.** Is inbound invoice and email text treated as untrusted data — or have you already seen a document “instruction” change a process?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Clerks follow footer instructions (new bank, “please approve”). |
| **1** | People know not to. No system help. |
| **2** | Policy says documents are not instructions. Not tested. |
| **3** | Bank and approval paths ignore document imperatives. Suspicious hidden text has a quality hold. |
| **4** | Explicit injection cases in any tool test pack; tools cannot fetch arbitrary URLs from invoices. |

**Why it matters.** Agents that read PDFs will meet hostile or accidental instructions. The control is architectural, not “better prompting.”

#### AR-06 — Ambition matches evidence

**Question.** Is the current AP-AI ambition (none / experiment / one pilot / “autonomous AP”) consistent with your other five domain scores — in this sitting, not in a strategy deck?

| Score | What it looks like in AP |
|---|---|
| **0** | Public commitment to autonomous AP or guaranteed savings. No measurement. |
| **1** | Multiple tools in flight. No single owner. Success = vendor slide. |
| **2** | One experiment. Success criteria unwritten. |
| **3** | Ambition is one bounded agent at observe/recommend. Success = miss log and evidence pack. |
| **4** | Written ambition: one slice, one owner, no execute until Measure. Board language matches this diagnostic. |

**Why it matters.** Over-ambition is a control risk. Under-ambition is a delay. This question is the honesty check.

**Domain 6 evidence note.** Artefacts: any existing tool charter, shadow/UAT pack, human-class note, identity list, injection/quality hold example, written ambition statement.

## Maturity model

Map **overall score** (average of 36 questions, 0.0–4.0) to one level. Use the **hard brakes** even if the average is higher.

| Level | Name | Overall score | What this organisation is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Unprepared | 0.0–0.7 | Path, data, and controls are not stable enough to supervise a tool. Do not buy an agent. Fix identity, SoD, duplicate, and channel inventory. |
| **2** | Experimental | 0.8–1.5 | Some process language exists. Measurement is thin. A shadow on historical invoices is the most you should attempt — and only on a slice. |
| **3** | Governed pilot | 1.6–2.3 | You can name owners, codes, and a first agent. Observe and recommend are in play. Prepare is conditional. Execute is not earned. |
| **4** | Earned scale | 2.4–3.1 | Evidence packs exist. One increment at a time (level *or* scope). Human classes hold under pressure. |
| **5** | Institutional | 3.2–4.0 | Agents are jobs in an operating system: versioned instructions, sampled execute, kill-switch drilled, measurement owned by Finance — not a vendor. Still not “autonomous payment.” |

**Hard brakes (override the average).**

| Condition | Effect |
|---|---|
| Domain 3 (Controls) average < 2.0 | Cap maturity at **Experimental**, regardless of overall. |
| CA-03 or CA-04 scored 0 | Cap at **Unprepared** for any money-adjacent tool. Fix duplicate and bank-change first. |
| Domain 2 (Data) average < 1.5 | Cap at **Unprepared** for live agents. Shadow on a static file is the ceiling. |
| More than 12 answers tagged `G` | Result is **indicative**. Do not use it to approve spend. |
| AR-06 scored 0 | Record a governance finding. Ambition is a risk, not a score to “work on later.” |

**What to do.** Read your level as a permission, not a grade.

**How.** Apply hard brakes after arithmetic.

**Who.** Challenge applies brakes. Scorer does not negotiate them away.

**What can go wrong.** Calling Level 3 “ready to scale” because a vendor uses that word.

**Control.** The table above is the only mapping. Do not add stars or traffic lights that contradict it.

**Measure.** Level, domain range, brakes applied (yes/no and which).

**Evidence.** This page, dated, with the six domain scores printed.

## Opportunity heatmap

**What to do.** Pick up to five candidate opportunities (invoice slices or exception families). Score each on four axes using *your* data. Plot volume × pain on the page; use control risk and data readiness as filters, not as decoration.

**How.**

For each candidate, score 1–5 on each axis. Use the rubric. If you lack data, score the axis **1** and mark `unknown`.

| Axis | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Volume** | Rare in your last quarter | Material weekly queue | Dominant share of invoices or exceptions in the chosen scope |
| **Pain** | Low age, low supplier noise, low overtime | Visible stall or rework | Aged, escalated, or consuming disproportionate minutes (from *your* EE-03, not a benchmark) |
| **Control risk** | Break cannot move cash or identity | Operational error (wrong park, slow chase) | Touches payment destination, duplicate pay, tax position, GR invention, or DoA |
| **Data readiness** | Cannot read the objects needed | Reads exist; stale or incomplete | Stable keys, readable PO/GR/vendor, evidence store for this slice |

**Plot.** X = Volume (1–5). Y = Pain (1–5). Each bubble is a candidate.

**Filter (do not skip).**

| If | Then |
|---|---|
| Control risk = 4 or 5 | Not a first-wave *execute* candidate. Eligible for observe/recommend only, or for a human-class packaging agent (e.g. Duplicate flag, Payment review later). |
| Data readiness ≤ 2 | Do not agentise. Fix the extract or interface. |
| Volume ≤ 2 and Pain ≤ 2 | Park. It is a pet project. |
| Volume ≥ 4, Pain ≥ 4, Control risk ≤ 3, Data readiness ≥ 4 | First-wave shortlist. Still starts at L0. |

**Measure.** Count of bubbles that survive the filter. If zero, the next step is data or control work, not a Starter Kit implementation.

## How to interpret the score

**What to do.** Produce a one-page read for the CFO. Use the pattern below. Do not average your way to comfort.

**How — the one-page script.**

1. **Overall.** “We scored **X.X / 4.0** (**N / 144**). Maturity **level name**. Brakes applied: [none / list]. Guess-tagged answers: [n].”
2. **Shape.** Print six domain scores. Name the lowest domain in one sentence: what is missing in AP terms.
3. **Permission.**  
   - Level 1 → no agent spend. Fix CA-03/CA-04/DS identity as applicable.  
   - Level 2 → historical or shadow only, one slice.  
   - Level 3 → Starter Kit; one agent; L0/L1.  
   - Level 4 → Professional operating system; still one increment.  
   - Level 5 → operate; do not relax human classes.
4. **First slice.** The single heatmap survivor you will actually staff. If none, say so.
5. **Not a finding of value.** The score does not say agents will pay for themselves. Deloitte’s 21% “clear, measurable ROI” figure is a reminder that deployment ≠ return. Cite it only as context.

**Decision table**

| If you see | Say | Do next |
|---|---|---|
| Controls < 2.0 | “We are not allowed to execute. We may not even be allowed to experiment on live invoices.” | Bank-change + duplicate + SoD work |
| Data < 1.5 | “We cannot measure or feed an agent honestly.” | Channel volume + invoice key + extract |
| Exceptions strong, process weak | “We know the pile; we do not know the path.” | As-done map before Triage |
| Agent readiness >> other domains | “Ambition is ahead of the shop.” | Freeze new tool spend; run this diagnostic’s hard brakes |
| All domains ~2 with few guesses | “Governed pilot is honest.” | Buy or use the Starter Kit; book a Monday workshop |
| High score, 15 guesses | “We performed confidence, not diagnosis.” | Two-hour evidence pull; re-score |

**Who.** Head of AP writes the page. Challenge signs “brakes correctly applied.” CFO receives one page plus the baseline worksheet.

**What can go wrong.** Sending the 36 rubrics without interpretation. Sending interpretation without domain scores.

**Control.** One page. Six numbers. One slice. One brake statement.

**Measure.** Whether the CFO can repeat the permission sentence without notes.

**Evidence.** The one-pager, dated.

## What this is NOT

This diagnostic is **not**:

- A guarantee of savings, ROI, or payback.
- A fraud-detection assessment or a statement that duplicates will be caught.
- A regulatory, tax, SOX, or statutory-compliance opinion.
- An accounting-accuracy certificate or an audit.
- Permission to release payments, change vendor bank data, grant policy exceptions, or settle legal disputes by model.
- A ranking of ERPs or vendors.
- A substitute for the Starter Kit (Tier 1) or the Professional operating system (Tier 2).
- Valid if weights were changed, if industry targets were typed into the baseline, or if one person scored without a challenge.
