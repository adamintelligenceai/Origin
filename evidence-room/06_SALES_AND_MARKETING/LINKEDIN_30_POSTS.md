## 1 — Insight  
The AP stack can be healthy while the agent layer is unofficial.

Capture extracts. Match applies a table. Workflow routes. A model writes a fluent note.

None of that is an operating system for agents. An OS has named roles, a packet that must exist before a recommendation is eligible, a register of permission, and a written hold on payment release.

If you cannot point to those four, you do not have agentic AP. You have activity.

---

## 2 — Insight  
“Touchless AP” is not a commissioning target.

It is a political sentence that collapses the difference between a clean-match class and a payment run.

A narrow, pre-approved action inside a written limit table may, one day, be earned. Payment authorisation is not in that set. If a vendor slide treats them as the same arrow, the slide is wrong.

---

## 3 — Insight  
Intake is not matching.

When one “AP agent” does both, you cannot tell whether yesterday’s error was capture, policy, or confidence theatre.

Separate the roles. Give each a human owner. Write what it will not do. Then you have something Audit can sample.

---

## 4 — Insight  
A flag is a hypothesis.

Duplicate and anomaly signals are useful when they create a review task. They become dangerous when someone names them “fraud detection” and lets the name do the work.

Clearing a high-value flag is a human control. The model does not get a gavel.

---

## 5 — Insight  
Prepare is not execute.

A complete packet — extract, worksheet, correspondence, owner, clock — can sit on a desk and still be unfinished work.

L2 in a serious autonomy model means the packet is ready. A named human, or a downstream control, still performs the act. If your programme uses “ready” as a synonym for “posted,” you have skipped a level.

---

## 6 — Insight  
The system of record question is two questions.

ERP remains the system of record for accounting.

Something else must be the system of record for work-object state: which agent, which human, which evidence URI, which SLA, which autonomy context.

If both answers are “the chat window,” you will not close cleanly.

---

## 7 — Insight  
Cost is a control.

Token spend, licence spend, and exception minutes per thousand invoices should be visible.

An agent that is expensive and wrong should be demoted or retired. Scaling it because the demo was fluent is how unofficial workforces grow.

This is not a savings guarantee. It is hygiene.

## 9 — Insight  
Delegation of authority is not a prompt.

A model can route a packet toward the person the table names. It cannot edit the table, invent a substitute approver, or treat “urgent” as a new limit.

If Agent 07 (or your equivalent) is allowed to rewrite DOA, you do not have an approval agent. You have a policy leak.

---

## 10 — Insight  
Supplier drafts are not company speech.

Let an agent assemble a query. Let a human send it — or approve the send.

Commercial commitments, settlement language, and anything that sounds like a concession do not belong in an unattended draft that “just went out.”

---

## 11 — Insight  
Root cause that implements itself is a different agent with no charter.

Clustering exceptions is observation. Proposing a process fix is a recommendation. Changing a tolerance table, a vendor master rule, or a plant’s receiving habit is a human decision.

Keep Agent 15 at propose. Give the process owner a written yes or no.

---

## 12 — Insight  
First-time match is an observed rate.

It is a useful number after you can produce match worksheets for a sample.

It is a harmful number when it appears on a business case as a promised lift from “AI.” You can move the rate by loosening the table. That is not intelligence. That is a policy change wearing a model’s face.

---

## 13 — Insight  
Internal follow-up fails when the fact pack is a nudge.

“Please look at this” is not a packet. A packet is the invoice reference, the break type, the last three actions, the owner of the missing receipt or PO, and the clock.

Agents can assemble that. Humans still owe a response. Orchestrator should age by named person, not by queue nickname.

---

## 14 — Insight  
Period-end attestation is not a checklist completion.

An AP close agent can drive the list and surface accrual candidates. The Controller still signs.

If your close deck starts saying “the agent attested,” stop the programme until the sentence is impossible.

## 16 — Insight  
Silent promotion is the usual accident.

Monday: the agent summarises exceptions.  
Thursday: someone lets it draft supplier mail.  
The following Tuesday: it is “allowed to park documents.”

None of those steps appeared on a register. Write the register. Review it quarterly. Treat prompt and model version changes as change-controlled configuration.

---

## 17 — Insight  
PO quality belongs in the AP picture as a signal, not a takeover.

Defective POs become AP exceptions. An agent can diagnose the defect class. Procurement still owns the PO.

If AP uses “AI” to rewrite buying policy, you have a governance problem that no matching engine will fix.

---

## 18 — Insight  
Goods receipt delays are not matching errors.

A match agent that invents a receipt to “clear the queue” has exceeded its charter, whatever the confidence score.

Missing GR is a different role, a different owner, and a different packet. Keep them apart and your exception mix becomes readable.

---

## 19 — Insight  
Vendor statements are a reconciliation, not a payment instruction.

An agent may propose a map from statement lines to open items. It may not treat an unmatched statement line as authority to add a payment.

Treasury and AP still own the proposal that goes to the run.

---

## 20 — Insight  
Reporting agents should cite defined sources.

A fluent narrative that cannot point to the extract, the date, and the filter is not a control pack. It is a story.

If you would not sign a manual pack with those properties, do not let a model sign it with tone.

---

## 21 — Insight  
The Orchestrator is not a second ERP.

It sequences work, records handoffs, and holds the SLA clock. It does not post invoices, change vendors, or release cash.

When vendors sell “one agent to run AP,” ask where posting, vendor master, and payment release actually sit. If the answer is “the same agent,” decline.

## 27 — Insight  
L4 is not a promotion for payment.

Managed autonomy, in a serious model, is exception-based oversight of a bounded set that was already allowed at L3.

It is not a new class of work. It is not “now it can pay.” If your ladder uses L4 as the payment rung, rebuild the ladder before you commission anything.

## 28 — Insight  
Exclusions are as important as duties.

The useful line in a charter is often the list of things the agent will not do: no bank details, no DOA edits, no external send, no invented receipts, no self-expansion of scope.

If a charter is only a list of hopes, it is a prompt.
