**Use:** Fridays in the 90-day system, or whenever the honest ask is the free assessment.  
**Tone:** still ~80% teaching. The Diagnostic is a product, not a “comment READY for the PDF”.  
**CTA:** Assess Your AP Agent Readiness → evidenceroom.ai/diagnostic  
**Never:** imply the Diagnostic scores savings, certifies SOX, or picks a vendor.

## D1 — Take a level, not a grade

Most teams who “have AI in AP” cannot say whether they are unscoped, tool-led, designed, governed, or operating.

Those are levels 0–4. A level is a coordinate. It is not a grade and it is not a business case.

The AP Agent Readiness Diagnostic is a structured way to take that coordinate in the environment you actually run.

Free. Completed locally. We do not want your invoices.

## D2 — Level 1 is the crowded room

A model is in trial. A vendor has a champion. The slide says intelligence.

Ownership is unclear. The fence is “we’ll see”. Pause is a vendor ticket.

That is Level 1. It is where most programmes sit. The Diagnostic is built to say so without theatre.

If you are there, your next work is a live-process map — not another tool.

## D3 — Questions it actually asks

The Diagnostic does not ask “Are you excited about AI?”

It asks whether you can name:

- the system of record  
- the invoice class you would fence first  
- the steward and the control owner  
- where evidence would live  
- what you would read in 30 days  
- who can stop the agent on a Tuesday  

If those answers are stories, the level will show it.

## D4 — It is allowed to tell you to wait

A serious assessment can recommend that you do not buy anything.

If you have no map of the Tuesday path, buying a library of agent designs is costume jewellery.

The Diagnostic will say “wait and map” when that is the honest next step. We will not override it with a coupon.

## D5 — The gap map

A single number is not enough. The Diagnostic marks six gaps: process, ownership, controls, evidence, measurement, change.

Present, partial, or missing.

Two “missing” columns tell you more than a vendor score that always lands on “ready to transform”.

Take the map. Then decide whether Starter, Professional, Team, or nothing is the rung.

## D6 — Shared services should take it too

If only the transformation office completes the Diagnostic, you will get the hopeful version.

Ask the shared-services supervisor who still owns exceptions to complete it separately. Compare the levels.

The disagreement is the finding.

## D7 — Audit can be in the room

Internal audit does not have to “approve AI”. They can take the Diagnostic as a reader: would we know how to ask for evidence?

If audit lands two levels below AP, do not average the scores. Fix the evidence gap before limited production.

The Diagnostic is not an audit opinion. It is a way to see that gap early.

## D8 — After you know your level

Level 0–1: map one class. Stay with the notes.  
Level 1 with a vendor on site: fence the first agent (Starter or Professional).  
Level 2 and you own the method: Professional.  
Several people must move: Team.  
The environment will not fit a toolkit: Custom application.

The Diagnostic is the sorting hat. It is not the operating system.

evidenceroom.ai/diagnostic

## D9 — A paragraph for the CFO

When you finish, you should be able to paste five sentences:

What we are (level).  
What is missing (two gaps).  
What we will not claim (savings, fraud, compliance, accuracy, autonomous payments, ROI).  
What we will map or fence next.  
What would make us stop.

If you cannot write those sentences, complete the Diagnostic again without performing.
