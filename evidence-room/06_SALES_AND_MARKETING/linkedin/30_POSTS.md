That layer sits *across* the ERP and AP stack you already have. It does not replace it.

Evidence Room publishes that system. First product: AP Agent OS.

## Post 02 — Tool-led

A vendor in the building is not an operating model.

Most “AI in AP” programmes are tool-led. A model is in trial. A slide says touchless. Exceptions still sit with the same six people. Nobody can say what the agent may not do.

That is Level 1. It is a crowded room. It is also a coordinate, not an insult.

Write the job before you extend the trial.

## Post 04 — Agent ≠ suite

Your AP automation suite is not an agent.

Capture, route, match, and archive are systems of record and workflow. They matter. They are not a responsibility model.

An agent, as we use the word, is a designed role that can propose or prepare work *inside a fence*, with evidence and a pause.

If a vendor uses “agent” for a chat window on the same workflow, keep the software if it is useful. Still write the fence.

---

## Post 05 — Fence

If you cannot name the invoice class, the amount, the vendor set, and the system, you do not have an agent.

You have a hope.

The fence is the most unfashionable artefact in Finance AI. It is also the one an auditor can read.

## Post 06 — Northline, scene 1

Northline Industrial Group is fictional. We say that every time so we are not tempted to invent a case study.

If Northline’s AP lead were asked to “turn on AI”, she would not start with a model. She would map the Tuesday path for PO invoices, write a purpose sentence a controller would sign, and refuse any agent that issues a payment instruction.

That is the scene. Your plants will differ.

## Post 07 — Evidence pack

“The model said so” is not evidence.

An evidence pack is dull: inputs, outputs, timestamp, version of the brief, human decision, where the file lives, who may read it.

If that pack does not exist, you do not have a governed agent. You have a conversation that will not survive a request from audit.

---

## Post 08 — Pause

Governance is the ability to stop.

If turning the agent off requires a steering committee, a vendor ticket, and a release window, you built a process you do not control.

Pause authority belongs to a named person, on a Tuesday afternoon, in one sentence.

---

## Post 09 — Measure without ROI

You can measure an AP agent without inventing a finance story.

Read: cycle time by class, exception age, human review rate, evidence completeness, fence-breaches, pause events.

Do not read, as a product claim: savings, ROI, fraud caught, “fully compliant”.

Ardent Partners and Forrester publish industry context. Cite them if you use them. They are not your baseline until you measure your own environment.

---

## Post 10 — Audit reader

Internal audit is not the enemy of agents.

Audit is the reason to write the brief in language that can be defended. Purpose, fence, control owner, evidence index, pause.

If a programme is designed so that audit “will never understand it”, the programme is not sophisticated. It is unfinished.

## Post 14 — Steward

Name a steward.

Not “the AP team”. A person who versions the brief, reads the log, and tells the truth when the fence is breached.

Without a steward, the agent belongs to the vendor’s customer-success motion. That is not an operating model.

---

## Post 15 — Control owner

The control owner is a different human from the steward, on purpose.

Steward: the job and the log.  
Control owner: the control objectives and the evidence.

If one person is both, write that down as a risk, not as efficiency.

---

## Post 16 — Review / AP ops

The people who work exceptions already know whether your map is a fantasy.

Sit with them before you sit with a model. Ask them to strike every step that does not happen on Tuesday.

If they are not in the design, they will be in the incident.

---

## Post 17 — Escalation list

An agent without an escalation list will resolve things it cannot see.

Write the states it must not finish: new vendor, amount breach, missing PO, payment instruction, control override, anything outside the fence.

Then name the human who receives those states. “The queue” is not a name.

---

## Post 18 — Stop conditions

Stop conditions are not drama. They are design.

Examples: fence-breach count above a threshold you set; evidence completeness below a threshold you set; the steward is on leave and no deputy exists.

If you have no stop conditions, you have an agent that is “always fine” — which is how quietly bad systems live.

---

## Post 19 — Change control

When the fence, the instructions, or the model changes, the brief gets a version.

If changes happen in a chat window and nowhere else, you will not be able to say what was live last Thursday.

Dull change control is a feature.

---

## Post 20 — Versioned brief

The agent brief is the contract among steward, control owner, reviewer, and executive reader.

It is one to three pages. It is dated. It can be read aloud.

If your “brief” is a slide titled Transformation, write a brief.

---

## Post 21 — Twelve minutes

An executive briefing on an AP agent should fit in twelve minutes:

- the purpose sentence  
- the fence  
- the three measures  
- what you will not claim  
- what turns it off  
- the ask: hold, continue limited, or stop  

If you need a forty-minute demo, you are not briefing. You are selling.

---

## Post 22 — Historical sample

Do not give an agent production traffic because a demo was clean.

Take a past sample of the fenced class. See what fails. Write it down. Change the brief or do not go live.

A historical sample is cheaper than a story.

## Post 28 — Second agent

The second agent is how programmes become soup.

Scale a second job only when the first fence is boringly stable: measures read, pause rehearsed, brief versioned.

Classify, flag, draft, pack — those jobs can wait. They should wait.

Never: pay, override, close the books, “detect all fraud”.
