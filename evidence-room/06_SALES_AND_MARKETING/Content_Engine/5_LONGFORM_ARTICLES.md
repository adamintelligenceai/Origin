## Article 1 — The AP Agent Operating Model

### Outline
1. The missing layer between AP suites and AI enthusiasm  
2. Five planes (systems, agents, humans, evidence, governance)  
3. Seven product questions  
4. Responsibility progression  
5. What “good” looks like in 90 days  
6. Closing: evidence over hype  

### Opening section

Accounts Payable does not lack software. Most mid-market and enterprise teams already own capture, workflow, and payment platforms. What they lack is an operating model for **agent authority**: a disciplined answer to what an AI agent may do, what evidence it must produce, who owns exceptions, and how responsibility expands without theatre.

Vendor roadmaps will keep shipping AI features. That is necessary and insufficient. Features without promotion rules become access rights justified by demos. Evidence Room names the gap plainly and treats agents as workforce roles that **earn** responsibility.

Industry climate underscores urgency without dictating your business case. Ardent Partners’ *State of ePayables 2025* reports peer average cost per invoice at **$9.84**, Best-in-Class at **$2.65**, exception rates at **18.4%**, STP at **35.4%**, and AI adoption at **44%**. Those figures are context. They are not a licence to paste ROI onto a slide.

The rest of this article defines the five planes of an AP agent operating model and the seven questions every agent must answer before it touches a production queue.

## Article 2 — Exception Taxonomy as Workforce Design

### Outline
1. Why inboxes defeat AI pilots  
2. A starter taxonomy (EX-* codes)  
3. Ownership and KPI treatment  
4. Heatmaps: clarity vs control risk  
5. How taxonomy enables Assist-mode agents  
6. Practical 30-day adoption  

### Opening section

Most AP AI conversations begin with capture accuracy or “touchless” aspiration. The operational truth is sterner: a material share of invoices still require human intervention, and peer exception rates remain elevated — **18.4%** in Ardent’s 2025 peer set. If exceptions live as unstructured inbox work, an agent will not remove burden; it will accelerate confusion.

Exception taxonomy is workforce design. Naming classes (data, match, policy, duplicate, master data, ambiguity, system) forces ownership, routing, and measurement. Without that spine, STP targets are theatre and dashboards stay ornamental.

This article proposes a starter taxonomy, shows how to keep “Other” from becoming a junk drawer, and explains why high-control-risk work must stay human even when clarity looks tempting.

## Article 3 — Measuring Earned Responsibility (Not Vanity Automation)

### Outline
1. Formulas before targets  
2. Leading vs lagging indicators  
3. Why industry BIC is not your KPI  
4. Promotion hold rules  
5. Dis-benefits and false flags  
6. Steering committee use of scorecards  

### Opening section

Finance leaders are right to ask for measurement. They are wrong when measurement becomes a hunt for a single guaranteed savings percentage. Agents should earn responsibility the way people do: against defined expectations, over a hold period, with demotion available when control fails.

Start with formulas. Assisted volume, exception rate on an in-scope slice, taxonomy coverage, evidence completeness, false flag rate, override rate, and stop-condition hits will tell you more about readiness than a cost-per-invoice fantasy extrapolated from Best-in-Class peers at **$2.65** versus averages at **$9.84**.

This article builds a KPI OS mindset for AP agents and shows how steering committees can review scorecards without slipping into hype.

## Article 4 — Stack Honesty: Agents Across the AP Platforms You Own

### Outline
1. Rip-and-replace is a different project  
2. Systems of process vs agent layer  
3. Integration and identity prerequisites  
4. Payment boundary as non-negotiable default  
5. How Evidence Room positions beside major suites  
6. Buyer checklist  

### Opening section

If your primary initiative is replacing Coupa, Tipalti, Basware, Stampli, Medius, HighRadius, Esker, AvidXchange, BILL, Ramp, SAP, Oracle, or Microsoft Dynamics, you need a platform programme — not an agent operating system brochure. Evidence Room is explicit about that boundary because stack honesty is a control issue, not a marketing preference.

The agent layer sits across systems of process. It classifies, drafts, assembles evidence, and flags risk. It does not become a shadow ERP. Payment execution remains human- and platform-owned in the default posture. Teams that blur that line confuse innovation with entitlements.

This article gives IT and Controllers a shared language for where agents plug in — and where they must not.

## Article 5 — From Diagnostic to Programme: A 90-Day Path

### Outline
1. Free diagnostic and gates  
2. Starter: one agent  
3. Pro: install the OS  
4. Team: people and cadence  
5. Custom: when facilitation is required  
6. What to refuse to claim at every step  

### Opening section

The fastest way to waste AI budget in Accounts Payable is to skip sequence. Teams jump from a keynote to multi-agent ambition without readiness gates, then wonder why Audit arrives late and angry.

A sober path exists: assess readiness; design one governed agent; install operating-system artefacts; scale with workshop, training, and steering; facilitate multi-entity blueprints only when complexity demands it. Commercially that maps to Free, Starter (**$79**), Pro (**$199**), Team (**$499**), and Custom (**$1,500–$3,000**).

This article walks the 90-day path and lists the claims you should refuse even when a vendor — or your board — asks for them.
