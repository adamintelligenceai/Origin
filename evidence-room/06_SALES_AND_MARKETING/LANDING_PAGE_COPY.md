## 3. Problem

### The stack is busy. The agent layer is undesigned.

Most AP organisations now have some combination of capture, match, workflow, and a model sitting in a side window. Someone has pasted a prompt. Someone has a vendor demo titled “touchless.”

What they rarely have:

- a named workforce of agents, as opposed to “the AP AI”
- a written packet that must exist before a recommendation is eligible
- a register of who is allowed to do what, at which autonomy level
- a sentence, in writing, that payment release is not an agent job
- a way to demote an agent that is expensive and wrong

The result is not “transformation.” The result is unofficial permission. Models speak in a confident tone. Teams treat tone as authority. Payment files still go out on Tuesdays.

**The failure is not the ERP.**  
The failure is treating a tool stack as if it were an operating system for agents.

## 4. Agentic AP — what that phrase should mean

“Agentic AP” is being used to mean “the model does the work.” That definition will not survive contact with a Controller.

In this method, agentic AP means:

1. **Work is objectified.** Every invoice, exception, statement line, and payment-proposal line has an owner, a status, an evidence URI, and a clock.
2. **Roles are named.** Sixteen agents, each with exclusions. Intake does not match. Matching does not invent a goods receipt. Duplicate & Anomaly does not issue a verdict.
3. **Autonomy is earned.** Observe, then recommend, then prepare. Execute-within-guardrails is rare and never applied to payment release.
4. **Proof precedes permission.** An incomplete packet does not travel. A complete packet still does not pay.
5. **Humans remain A.** Agents may be R for preparation. A named person owns the outcome.

If a programme cannot say those five things out loud, it is not agentic. It is unofficial.

## 7. Responsibility model

### Agents prepare. Humans own.

| Activity | Agent may | Human must |
|---|---|---|
| Capture and extract | Recommend a structured record | Own AP operations quality |
| Match | Apply the published tolerance table | Own the match policy; resolve policy exceptions |
| Exception | Classify, assign, clock | Resolve, write off, concede |
| Supplier query | Draft | Send or approve sending; make no commercial commitment in a draft |
| Duplicate / anomaly | Flag a hypothesis | Clear, confirm, or escalate — especially above threshold |
| Payment proposal | Annotate | Authorise and release (dual, or as policy requires) |
| Close | Drive the checklist; list accrual candidates | Attest |
| Scope of an agent | Never expand itself | Promote, demote, or retire on evidence |

One human owner per agent. Accountability does not sit with “the SSC” or “Finance.” It sits with a named role.

If an action is not listed in the charter, the agent does not do it.

## 8. KPI — measure the layer, not a fantasy return

Boards are being shown savings slides for programmes that cannot produce a match worksheet. We will not write those slides.

**Use these measures instead.** They describe whether the layer is real.

| Measure | Intent |
|---|---|
| Every inbound invoice has a work object inside the Intake SLA | Capture is complete enough to govern |
| Validation fail reasons are coded | Exception mix can be analysed |
| Match worksheets exist for a defined sample | Matching is inspectable |
| Duplicate flags have a recorded clear / confirm / escalate outcome | No silent ignore |
| Orchestrator shows aging by named owner, not only by queue | Management can see the work |
| No payment released without the required human approvers | Control held |
| Autonomy register shows every live agent and its level | No silent promotion |
| Cost per 1,000 invoices (licence + model + exception minutes) is recorded | Cost is visible |

If first-time match or cycle time later moves, that is an observed outcome. It is not a commissioning promise, and it is not why the OS exists.

## 9. Governance

### Permission is a calendar, not a vibe.

- **Weekly:** exception mix, SLA breaches, flags awaiting a human, model-cost outliers.
- **Monthly:** tolerance table, DOA substitutes, supplier-query templates, limit-table edits — all human.
- **Quarterly:** autonomy recertification. Sample packets. Retire or demote what is expensive and wrong.
- **On change:** prompts, model versions, and connector mappings are change-controlled configuration, not Slack folklore.
- **On payment runs:** Agent 12’s notes are an input. Dual human approval is the act.
- **With Audit:** observer at day 30 and at each promotion above L1. Not the operator.

Governance here is not a claim that you are compliant. It is the habit of making permission inspectable.

## 10. Roadmap (yours, not ours)

### Ninety days to a designed layer — not to unsupervised AP

**Days 1–14 — Observe**  
Appoint the AP Process Owner and the AP Controls Lead. Charter Wave 1 only: Intake, Validation, Matching, Triage, Duplicate & Anomaly, Orchestrator. Stand them at L0. Shadow reports. No recommendations in the system of record.

**Days 15–45 — Recommend**  
Promote individually to L1 when the evidence gates are met. Run the human-vs-agent framework on ten exceptions and one payment run. Invite Internal Audit as observer.

**Days 46–75 — Prepare**  
Selected object types may move to L2: a complete packet, a named human, no execution by the agent. Open Wave 2 agents the same way. Do not open Payment Proposal Review or Close above L1.

**Days 76–90 — Recertify**  
Publish the autonomy register. Record cost per thousand invoices. Decide, in writing, that nothing payment-adjacent will be discussed for L3.

Wave 4 (earned execute on a narrow clean-match class) is optional, gated, and still not payment. Many organisations should never take it.

## 14. FAQ

**Is this an AP automation product?**  
No. It is the operating system for the agent layer. Keep your automation. Design the layer.

**Will this detect fraud?**  
No. Duplicate & Anomaly flags hypotheses. Humans clear them. A flag is not a verdict.

**Will this make us compliant?**  
No. It produces evidence. Your control owner decides sufficiency.

**Do you guarantee results?**  
No. We do not guarantee savings, cycle time, accuracy, or ROI.

**Is this just documents?**  
It is a licensed operating system in document form: roles, rights, evidence, refusals, and a ninety-day path. Implementation still happens in your stack, by your people.

**Can one person buy it for a whole shared-service centre?**  
Buy Team if several named practitioners will share and edit the system. Professional is one practitioner.

**Do you take our invoice data?**  
Not as a condition of delivery. Do not upload production files to our forms.

**Why “Evidence Room”?**  
Because proof is stored, and permission is granted elsewhere, by a named human. We are not the forensic-animation firm Evidence Room LLC.
