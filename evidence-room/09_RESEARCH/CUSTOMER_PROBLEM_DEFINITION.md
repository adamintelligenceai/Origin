# Customer Problem Definition — Evidence Room — AP Agent OS

**Lockup:** EVIDENCE ROOM — AP AGENT OS  
**Brand idea:** Proof before permission.  
**Date:** 20 September 2026  
**Not:** A persona-fiction novel. Northline Industrials remains a **fictional** operating example when used. It is not a customer.

This file defines who has the problem, what job they are hiring a method to do, who must say yes, and what useful work looks like from Friday afternoon to Monday morning.

---

## 1. The problem in one paragraph

Accounts Payable already has a stack: ERP, capture, match, workflow, mailbox, payment proposal, bank. Leadership has now asked the function to “put AI on AP.” Someone pastes a prompt. Someone turns on a vendor copilot. Someone books a demo titled “touchless.”

What the organisation rarely has is a **designed agent layer**: named roles, a packet that must exist before a recommendation is eligible, a register of autonomy, and a sentence in writing that payment release is not an agent job.

The failure is not usually the ERP. The failure is treating a tool stack as if it were an operating system for agents. The result is unofficial permission. Models speak fluently. Teams treat fluency as authority. Payment files still go out on Tuesdays.

Evidence Room sells the missing layer’s specification. It does not process the invoices.

## 2. Ideal customer profile (ICP)

### 2.1 Firmographic (centre of gravity)

| Attribute | In | Out |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Mid-market to lower enterprise; enough invoice volume that AP is a named team, not one person with a drawer | Sole trader; no AP process at all |
| Stack | Has, or is buying, an ERP **and** at least one of: capture, match, workflow, RPA, a copilot | Wants a product that *replaces* SAP / Oracle / NetSuite / the AP team |
| Complexity | PO and non-PO mix; more than one entity or more than one intake channel is enough | Purely personal bookkeeping |
| Trigger | A mandate to use AI in finance; a vendor renewal; an audit question; a near-miss payment; a transformation slide that says “agentic AP” | A mandate to guarantee savings this quarter as the only success metric |
| Geography | Written in plain finance English; usable globally as a method | Anyone needing localised legal, tax, or e-invoicing advice as the product |

We do not invent a TAM percentage. We do not need one to know the buyer.

### 2.2 The practitioner (user-buyer)

The person who will **do the weekend work** and keep the charters:

- Finance Transformation lead  
- AP Manager / AP Process Owner  
- Controller or Assistant Controller — Payables  
- Shared-service AP lead  

Often a **trio**: Transformation (pen), AP (operations), Controls (holds). Team licence exists so those pens can share one system.

### 2.3 The institution (economic buyer)

The organisation that will appear on the licence: a named company or shared-service centre. Individual and Professional are one practitioner. Team is a working group. Custom Blueprint is a facilitated design against *their* stack.

### 2.4 Anti-ICP (say no)

- Wants unsupervised payment release this quarter.  
- Needs a vendor to **guarantee** savings, ROI, fraud coverage, or compliance.  
- Thinks the product is hosted AP software or an ERP connector.  
- Wants a prompt pack to paste before a demo.  
- Will not name a human owner for an agent.  
- Is shopping to **replace** the AP team or the ERP.

A polite no is cheaper than a refund argument.

## 3. Jobs to be done

### 3.1 Core JTBD

> When my organisation is about to put AI agents on invoice-to-pay, I want a written operating system for that layer — roles, evidence, permission, and human-held payment — so that I can commission work without granting unofficial authority.

### 3.2 Job stories

| When | I want to | So that |
|---|---|---|
| A sponsor asks “when will AP be agentic?” | Point to sixteen named roles and a first wave at L0/L1 | I do not answer with a feature flag |
| A vendor demo auto-releases under a threshold | Put a written hold on the table | Cash movement stays a named human control |
| Internal Audit asks to see the AI | Open packets, not a chat log | Inspection is possible |
| Two tools each have a copilot | Specify one workforce across them | We do not run two unofficial AP AIs |
| An exception sits in an inbox | Code it, own it, clock it | Aging is by person, not by “the bot” |
| Someone pastes “detect fraud” into a prompt | Replace it with flag / hypothesis / human clearance | We do not write a verdict we cannot stand behind |
| Budget asks for ROI | Refuse to invent it; show layer-cost language | We do not buy a guarantee we cannot keep |
| Monday’s payment run is prepared | Let Agent 12 annotate only | Two humans still release |

### 3.3 Success the buyer can defend (not our guarantee)

Success in the first cycle is **operating discipline**:

- Wave 1 named (Intake, Validation, Matching, Triage, Duplicate & Anomaly, Orchestrator).  
- Owners named.  
- Evidence standard written.  
- Autonomy register at L0/L1.  
- Payment sentence true in policy **and** in the system.  
- Internal Audit invited as observer by day 30.

If first-time match or cycle time later moves, that is an **observed** local outcome. It is not why they bought, and it is not something we promise.

### 3.4 Jobs we refuse to be hired for

- Detect fraud.  
- Make the organisation compliant.  
- Authorise or transmit payment.  
- Guarantee accounting accuracy.  
- Produce a board savings number.  
- Replace Coupa, SAP, or the shared-service team.

## 4. Buying committee

| Role | Typical title | What they fear | What they must hear | Vote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Champion** | Finance Transformation lead; AP Manager | Another framework; looking naïve in front of a vendor | Wave 1 is six agents at observe/recommend. You name owners. Files, not a new platform. | Proposes |
| **Economic buyer** | Controller, CAO, sometimes CFO | Silent autonomy; a second system of record; a savings story that will be asked for later | Payment authorisation stays human. ERP stays the accounting system of record. No ROI promise from us. | Approves spend |
| **Control owner** | AP Controls Lead; Internal Audit | A black box; “the AI caught fraud”; unauditable promotion | Packets, sampling, flags as hypotheses, Audit as observer — not operator | Can veto go-live |
| **IT / ERP owner** | ERP lead; IT finance systems | Integration debt; another vendor in the landscape | No connector required to begin. Architecture is ERP-agnostic. | Technical assent |
| **Treasury / Payments** | Treasurer; AP Payments Lead | Agent-transmitted bank files; vendor-master drift | Agent 12 annotates. Humans release. Bank-detail change is dual-human. | Veto on payment design |
| **InfoSec / Legal** | CISO; counsel | Unofficial models on invoice images; uncontrolled prompts | Untrusted-content rules, least privilege, change control. We are not their model host. | Conditional go |
| **Procurement ops** | Procurement operations | AP dictating how to buy | Agent 06 diagnoses PO defects; Procurement owns the PO | Adjacent |
| **Users** | Processors, exception desk, plant finance liaison | Sixteen bots dumped on Monday; job-theatre | They remain Accountable. Agents prepare and recommend. | Adoption |

**Minimum viable committee for a Professional purchase:** champion + economic buyer awareness.  
**Minimum for Team:** champion + Controls + one other (Audit or Transformation).  
**Minimum for Blueprint:** champion, economic buyer, and a named AP Process Owner on the application.

A purchase that hides from Internal Audit is a future incident.

## 5. Friday-to-Monday journey

This is the **buyer’s** first useful cycle. It matches `00_READ_ME/README.md` and `QUICK_START.md`. It is not a software install.

### Friday afternoon — see the layer you have (60–90 minutes)

1. Read the suite contract: what it is, what it refuses.  
2. Complete the diagnostic with **last quarter’s facts**, not hopes.  
3. Score; do not average away a zero on payment control or vendor-bank dual control.  
4. Write the maturity band in one sentence.

**Exit artefact:** a band, not a certificate.

### Saturday — apply the rights table (2–3 hours)

5. Take the last **ten exceptions** and the last **payment run**.  
6. Classify each decision. Record what a vendor demo would have auto-released.  
7. Charter **Wave 1 only** at L0 or L1.  
8. Write the payment sentence where the team can see it: **Agent 12 annotates. Humans release.**

**Exit artefact:** six draft charters and a marked-up payment proposal.

### Sunday — name humans and sampling (90 minutes)

9. One owner and one backup per Wave 1 agent. If you cannot name them, do not commission.  
10. Draft the first-week sampling plan (coverage, coded fails, match worksheets, flag outcomes).  
11. Put Phase 0 on a calendar. Do not build a savings slide.

**Exit artefact:** owner list + calendar holds.

### Monday morning — make it institutional (45 minutes)

12. Circulate a one-page note: band, Wave 1 names, owners, payment hold, request for a 30-day control review with Internal Audit as observer.  
13. Stand agents at **L0 (shadow)** only if owners, object types, and the evidence standard are written.  
14. Refuse “just turn on auto-post” and “just release under $5k.”

**Exit artefact:** a note the Controller can defend.

That is a complete first cycle. Promotion is later and evidence-gated. Nothing in this journey requires uploading invoices to Evidence Room.

|---|---|

| “We need guaranteed outcomes” | Then this is the wrong product. We will not write the guarantee. |

## 7. Problem we do **not** claim to own

McKinsey’s independent research (ledger R-04–R-17) describes a world of **rising gen-AI use** and **thin scaling**, with workflow redesign as a high-performer differentiator and inaccuracy as a common negative consequence. That is **context** for why an operating system is a reasonable product. It is not a claim that the buyer is in McKinsey’s 6% high-performer set, or that AP cost will move from $12.88 to $2.78.

Ardent’s Best-in-Class vs All Others split (R-01–R-03) describes **other organisations in a survey**. It is not the buyer’s baseline and not our deliverable.
