# Evidence Standard — Customer-Facing Claims

**Lockup:** EVIDENCE ROOM — AP AGENT OS  
**Date:** 20 September 2026  
**Controls:** `RESEARCH_LEDGER.md` is the only quantitative source file. `10_LEGAL_AND_LICENSING/DISCLAIMER.md` wins if copy conflicts.  
**Audience:** Anyone writing site, ads, email, LinkedIn, decks, Blueprint conversations, or affiliate notes.

This standard is how a researched fact is allowed to appear in public. It is not a permission to add new facts.

---

## 1. Three classes of sentence

| Class | What it is | May appear in customer copy? |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Product fact** | Something the zip contains (sixteen agents, L0–L4, payment hold) | Yes. Prefer the wording in `PRODUCT_COPY.md`. |
| **B — Researched third-party fact** | A row in `RESEARCH_LEDGER.md` | Yes, **only** in an approved pattern below, with independence visible. |
| **C — Editorial analysis** | Judgement (e.g. “vendors sell software, not operating models”) | Yes, if labelled as our view or clearly our positioning — never dressed as a study. |

**Forbidden class D:** A number that is not in the ledger. Invented averages. Northline figures as benchmarks. “Customers save…” We have no customer-outcome study.

---

## 2. Hard bans (repeat from the brand system)

Do not claim, imply, or let a partner claim:

- guaranteed savings, cost-takeout, or ROI  
- fraud detection or fraud prevention as a property of the OS or of an agent  
- regulatory compliance as an outcome of using the toolkit  
- accounting accuracy as a property of an agent  
- autonomous payment safety  
- “touchless AP” as a committed state  
- replacement of the AP team, Controller, or ERP  
- that Evidence Room is a law firm, audit firm, or payment institution  

If a researched figure would become persuasive only by violating a ban, **do not use the figure**.

---

## 3. Independence must travel with the number

When a Class B sentence is used, the reader must be able to see the hop.

| Ledger independence | Public treatment |
|---|---|
| `independent` | Name McKinsey (or the independent org), publication, and year. |
| `vendor` | Name the vendor. “Lemon Squeezy’s public pricing page states…” Never “industry fees are…” |
| `vendor-citing-independent` | Name **both**: “Ardent Partners (*State of ePayables 2024*), as cited by Tipalti…” Do not drop Tipalti. Do not say “independent research shows” as if we commissioned Ardent. |
| `unverified` | Do not publish. |

Do not say “studies show” or “analysts agree.”

## 4. Approved patterns (use or stay close)

These are the only quantitative patterns authorised for customer-facing use. Shorten for character limits, but do not drop the caveat that makes the sentence honest.

### 4.1 Ardent cost and time (R-01, R-02)

**Allowed**

> Ardent Partners’ *State of ePayables 2024* reports Best-in-Class all-inclusive cost to process a single invoice of $2.78 versus $12.88 for all other organisations in that study, and 3.1 versus 17.4 days. We cite this via Tipalti’s public article, which links the Ardent report (also distributed via Bottomline). Best-in-Class in that research is a maturity class — the 20% with the lowest cost and shortest cycle time — not a result of using Evidence Room.

**Not allowed**

- “Best-in-class AP costs $2.78 with AI — that’s what our OS delivers.”  
- “Cut processing from 17.4 days to 3.1.”  
- “Laggards pay $12.88; our customers pay $2.78.”  
- Using Tipalti’s “without AI” gloss as if it were Ardent’s column title, unless you also say that the Ardent table is Best-in-Class vs All Others.

**Better, often:** do not use the numbers at all. Use the product sentence: measure *your* cost per thousand invoices; do not import Ardent’s.

### 4.2 Ardent exceptions (R-03)

**Allowed**

> Tipalti cites Ardent Partners’ *AP Metrics that Matter 2025* for invoice exception rates of 22% and 9%. In Ardent’s *State of ePayables 2024* table, those same percentages appear as All Others (22.0%) versus Best-in-Class (9.0%) — a class comparison, not a measured before/after of a single automation project, and not an Evidence Room outcome.

**Not allowed**

- “Automation drops exceptions from 22% to 9%.” (causal, unsourced as a law)  
- “Our agents cut exceptions to 9%.”

**Default:** skip the number. Talk about **coded** exceptions and a mix you can analyse.

### 4.3 McKinsey CFO survey (R-04, R-05)

**Allowed**

> In McKinsey’s 3 November 2025 article *How finance teams are putting AI to work today*, 44% of 102 CFOs said they used generative AI for more than five use cases in 2025, up from 7% in the prior survey. 65% said their organisations would increase gen-AI investment in 2025. Breadth of use is not the same as a scaled, governed operating model.

**Not allowed**

- “44% of finance teams have scaled agentic AP.”  
- Combining 44% with the 1,993-person sample as if they were one poll.

### 4.4 McKinsey State of AI — adoption vs scale (R-06, R-09–R-12)

**Allowed**

> McKinsey’s *State of AI in 2025* (survey 25 June–29 July 2025, n=1,993 in 105 nations) finds 88% regular AI use in at least one function (from 78%), while nearly two-thirds of respondents say their organisations have not yet begun scaling AI across the enterprise. 62% are at least experimenting with agents; 23% are scaling an agentic system somewhere; no more than 10% are scaling agents in any given function.

**Not allowed**

- “Most companies are scaling agents.”  
- “88% have AP agents.”  
- Inventing an AP-function percentage.

### 4.5 McKinsey impact and risk (R-13–R-17)

**Allowed**

> In the same McKinsey 2025 survey, 39% report any enterprise EBIT impact from AI; about 6% meet McKinsey’s high-performer definition (5%+ EBIT impact and “significant” value). McKinsey finds workflow redesign is a top differentiator of those high performers. 51% of respondents from organisations using AI report at least one negative consequence; nearly one-third of all respondents report consequences from inaccuracy.

**Use this to justify restraint**, not to promise the buyer will join the 6%.

**Not allowed**

- “Join the 6%.”  
- “Workflow redesign with our toolkit produces EBIT.”  
- Rounding “nearly one-third” to a fake exact percent.

### 4.6 McKinsey time and biotech case (R-07, R-08)

**Allowed, rarely, in long-form only**

> McKinsey reports observing 20–30% less time spent crunching data in a handful of finance functions where AI was adopted robustly — an observation, not the CFO survey result, and not AP-specific.

> McKinsey describes a global biotech case in which an agentic invoice-to-contract system identified contract leakage of approximately 4% of spend. That is one case. Identified leakage is not recovered cash. It is not an Evidence Room customer.

**Not allowed in ads, hero, or pricing pages.** Too easy to become a promise. The hypothetical $1 billion / $40 million sentence **does not appear** in our customer copy.

### 4.7 Lemon Squeezy and Stripe (R-18–R-21)

**Allowed (operations and checkout, not AP outcomes)**

> Checkout is processed by Lemon Squeezy as merchant of record. Lemon Squeezy’s public pricing page (accessed 20 September 2026) states a platform fee of 5% + 50¢ per transaction. Additional fees may apply.

> Stripe announced its acquisition of Lemon Squeezy on 26 July 2024. Stripe’s Managed Payments documentation and 2026 changelog exist; they are a strategic option, not our current checkout.

**Not allowed**

- Publishing the full fee stack on the marketing homepage.  
- “No tax ever.”  
- “We are on Stripe Managed Payments” unless that is operationally true.

## 5. Attribution block (resources and decks)

At the foot of any piece that uses Class B numbers:

> Figures are from the named publications, accessed 20 September 2026 unless a later access date is shown. Independence labels and caveats: Evidence Room Research Ledger. They are not Evidence Room customer results and not a promise of savings, cycle time, exception rate, EBIT, or accuracy.

One block per URL path is enough. Do not footnote-wash a banned claim.

---

## 6. Charts and carousels

`06_SALES_AND_MARKETING/EXECUTIVE_CHARTS_10.md` may visualise **only** ledger rows, each with:

- title that names the source year  
- axis or caption that states Best-in-Class vs All Others, or survey n, as applicable  
- a visible “not an Evidence Room result” line  

Do not put $2.78 and $199 Professional on the same graphic as if the toolkit produced the $2.78.

---

## 7. Northline and illustrations

Northline Industrials is fictional. Any dollar, rate, or volume attached to Northline is **illustrative**. The sentence that contains the number must say so. Northline may never appear in a chart that also carries Ardent or McKinsey figures.

---

## 8. Review checklist (before publish)

1. Is every number in the ledger? If no, remove it.  
2. Is the independence hop visible?  
3. Does the sentence still work if the reader assumes we **cannot** promise an outcome?  
4. Would Internal Audit hear a certificate we did not issue?  
5. Would a Controller hear that we authorise payment, detect fraud, or guarantee accuracy?  
6. Did an affiliate drop “as cited by Tipalti” or “Best-in-Class is a maturity class”?  
7. Does the Disclaimer still control?

If any check fails, the piece does not ship.

---

## 9. Who may authorise an exception

Only a written ledger amendment (new row: claim, value, source, date, URL, independence, exact context, caveats) plus a re-read of this standard. Slack is not an amendment.
