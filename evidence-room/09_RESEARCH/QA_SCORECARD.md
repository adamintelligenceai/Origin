# QA Scorecard — Evidence Room — AP Agent OS

**Lockup:** EVIDENCE ROOM — AP AGENT OS

**Edition claimed:** 1.0.0 working  
**Method:** Honest desk review of what is **on disk** in `/workspace/evidence-room` plus the publisher files. Promised-but-absent folders are scored as gaps, not as if they existed.

## 2. Dimension notes

### 2.1 Content — 8

**What is strong**

- Agent library (01–16), stack overview, autonomy progression, human-vs-agent framework.  
- Process-mapping methodology, exception taxonomy and trees, governance standard and control matrix.  
- KPI language that refuses ROI theatre.  
- Testing: historical, shadow, pilot, UAT, scripts.  
- Commercial pack: brand, product, landing, IA, email, LinkedIn, articles, calendar.  
- Store and fee notes that match Lemon Squeezy’s public card (ledger R-18–R-21).  
- Legal drafts with the right refusals.  
- This research folder: ledger, competitors, ICP, claim standard, domain note.  
- Scale roadmap that **withholds** AR/Close/Treasury/etc.

**What is weak**

- `00_READ_ME/PRODUCT_INDEX.md` lists `01_FREE_AP_AI_READINESS/`, `02_AP_AGENT_STARTER/`, `03_…/Business_Case/`, `03_…/Templates/`, `04_AP_AGENT_OS_TEAM/`, `05_CUSTOM_BLUEPRINT/` as if edition 1.0.0 contained them. On this disk at scoring time, **those trees were not present** (or not complete). Content score will rise when those files actually exist and match the index.  
- No independently authored customer stories (correct — we must not invent them — but the pack then has only Northline fiction).  
- Research ledger is intentionally thin (no extra Ardent metrics). That is discipline, not a library of AP benchmarks.

### 2.2 Practicality — 7

A Controller can do useful work this weekend **if** they have Professional-level files and print the charter template. The journey is sequenced (diagnose → rights table → Wave 1 → shadow).

Missing practicality: fillable diagnostic scorecard, cost-per-invoice workbook, autonomy-register spreadsheet, steering slides a human can project on Monday. Those are specified in prose and not generated. A buyer who paid $199 for “documents” can still work; a buyer who expected Excel will feel the gap.

### 2.3 Specificity — 9

This is the suite’s best trait. Agents have IDs, default autonomy, owners, exclusions. Payment is a hard ceiling. Duplicate & Anomaly is a flag, not a verdict. Exception codes are named. Northline examples are concrete and labelled fictional. Banned-phrase lists are specific enough to edit copy against.

One point reserved: some Team / Blueprint / Business_Case titles in the index are more specific than the files we can open.

### 2.4 Design — 3

`BRAND_SYSTEM.md` gives paper/graphite/signal-red direction and a “room with a signature gap” mark concept. That is not a design system.

**Not generated**

- PDF covers or typeset toolkit  
- Carousel images (copy exists; art does not)  
- Executive chart images (specs exist)  
- Website HTML/CSS  
- Favicon / lockup artwork (do not use the eToro Origin favicon in the repo root)  
- Printable marketplace derivatives as actual PDFs  

Until a designer ships those, design stays a verbal intention.

### 2.5 Credibility — 7

**Raises the score:** Disclaimer controls marketing. Ledger labels Tipalti→Ardent as `vendor-citing-independent` and refuses to recast Best-in-Class vs All Others as a before/after. McKinsey figures carry sample, dates, and “not our result.” No fake testimonials. Trademark collision is disclosed.

**Lowers the score:** No live customers. No counsel sign-off. Domain not registered. Northline could be misread if a partner strips the word “fictional.” Version history slightly looser than the ledger on Ardent wording (“electronic” / “laggards”) — copy should follow the ledger, not the looser line.

### 2.6 Differentiation — 9

Almost every commercial file repeats: keep the stack; design the layer; prompt ≠ OS; payment stays human. Competitor review names the usual suites and states we are not in their category. HighRadius is correctly flagged as a **language** collision, not a bake-off.

Risk: a marketplace listing that drops the lockup and looks like a prompt pack. Derivatives file warns; enforcement is future work.

### 2.7 Coherence — 7

Voice (intelligent, concise, non-hype) holds from agent specs through legal drafts. Prices match ($0 / $79 / $199 / $499 / $1,500–$3,000). Autonomy model does not contradict itself.

Coherence breaks where **navigation is ahead of files** (index vs disk) and where **research did not exist** until this folder. Cross-links to `09_RESEARCH/` are new; older commercial files do not yet point at the ledger. They should, in a patch, or writers will keep quoting Tipalti without the Best-in-Class caveat.

### 2.8 Usability — 6

For an author or a highly literate practitioner, Markdown in folders is usable. For a buyer on a phone Friday night, it is not. There is no HTML START HERE, no single PDF, no search, no in-file progress checklist that stores answers.

Licence-to-folder rules are clear in README **once those folders exist**.

### 2.9 Commercial value — 7

Enough to run a **quiet launch** of a digital toolkit: offer ladder, checkout copy, fee model, email sequence, 90-day calendar, affiliate-do-nots. Blueprint is correctly gated.

Not enough to look like a finished company: no live evidenceroom.ai, no LS products configured, no designed lead magnet, no counsel-final ToS on a URL. Financial model is platform-net only; counsel and design costs will dominate early.
