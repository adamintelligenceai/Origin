The problem

The stack is busy. The agent layer is undesigned.

Most AP organisations have capture, match, workflow, and a model in a side window. Someone has pasted a prompt. Someone has a vendor demo titled “touchless.”

What they rarely have: a named workforce of agents, a packet that must exist before a recommendation is eligible, a register of who may do what, and a sentence — in writing — that payment release is not an agent job.

The failure is not the ERP. The failure is treating a tool stack as if it were an operating system for agents.

Hard hold

ERP remains the system of record for accounting. Named humans remain the system of record for cash.

Payment authorisation, vendor bank changes, new-vendor approval, DOA edits, and period-close attestation stay human. Agents may observe, recommend, or prepare. They do not release.

What the public record actually says

McKinsey’s 2025 State of AI survey (n=1,993) found 88% of organisations regularly use AI in at least one function, and 62% are at least experimenting with agents — yet nearly two-thirds have not begun scaling AI across the enterprise. Only 23% report scaling an agentic system somewhere; no more than 10% are scaling agents in any given function.

High performers redesign workflows. That is the job of an operating system, not a prompt.
